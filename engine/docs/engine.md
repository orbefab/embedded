# Engine

This page describes the engine at its `engine_version`. It is not part of the netlist or the run folder format. [netlist.md](netlist.md) says what a document means, and this page says how the engine computes it. A change here moves `engine_version`, as [versions.md](versions.md) says.

A run folder does not depend on the target the engine was built for, because no float result that reaches it is left to the platform. The `f64` methods `exp`, `ln` and `cbrt` call the platform's math library, and two platforms can round their last bits differently. The engine calls the `libm` crate for them instead, which computes each one in Rust from operations IEEE 754 rounds the same way everywhere. `sqrt` stays the `f64` method, because IEEE 754 rounds it exactly. `clippy.toml` bans the `f64` and `f32` methods whose result depends on the platform, tests included, so a test's closed form is held to the same rule. Every example's run folder is the same on x86-64 Linux and on `wasm32-unknown-unknown`, `created_at` aside. No other target was measured. A target whose `f64` arithmetic is not IEEE 754 binary64, such as 32-bit x86 without SSE2, is outside this.

## Transient steps

The run solves the operating point at t = 0, then steps to `stop_ps`. Every switch edge, every source jump, and every potentiometer move before `stop_ps` is a breakpoint, and so is every stop of an active chip's core, as [Active chips](#active-chips) says. So is 1 ns after t = 0, and 1 ns after each edge or jump, when that time is before `stop_ps`. A step lands on each breakpoint and on `stop_ps`. A row time is not a breakpoint. Only the row times are written to the trace, as [Rows](#rows) says. Every accepted step also feeds the envelope. A row closes the interval open since the previous row. A step that is not a row folds into that interval. A restart solve, a step the error control refused, and a probe locating a chip event are not samples. The accepted step the probe lands is.

At each switch edge or source jump the step's solution holds the old contact or level. Then the run solves once more at that time, with the new contacts and levels in force, every capacitor held at its voltage, the way an `initial` voltage is held at t = 0, and every inductor held at its current. This restart solve gives the currents just after the change. It is never a row, and the next step starts from it. Each held voltage moves by the capacitor's current times 1 fs over its capacitance. Each held current moves by the inductance's voltage times 1 fs over its inductance. That hold is the backward Euler companion of a 1 fs step, as a capacitor's is. A jump at `stop_ps` gets no restart. A `steps` event or a potentiometer move is a jump under that rule.

The run reads each part's edges one at a time, in time order, so it never lists a schedule whole. A dense schedule costs steps, not rows. The row cap counts grid rows only. A run accepts at most 100000000 steps, not counting t = 0. The limit sits far above the steps a run whose rows fit needs on ordinary circuits. At this step policy a 1 MHz pin into a 10 ns load costs about 170000 steps per simulated ms. The limit allows about 590 ms of such a pin. The step policy, not this limit, bounds an MCU-rate run. Reaching the limit before `stop_ps` ends the run with `solver.step_limit` at the last accepted time.

Solver tests that need every accepted step use `simulate_steps`, behind the test-only `step-rows` feature. A `sim` build does not have it. The test-only step-rows mode can also stop mid-run with `trace.too_large`, with `t_ps` the step that did not fit, keeping its rows.

## Rows

A row is a probe, never a landing. It is solved from the last accepted step to the row time, with the method, the chip states and the drive images the next step uses, the way a probe locating a chip event is. The run never accepts it. When a step lands at a row's time anyway, on an edge, a core stop or `stop_ps`, the row is that step, which is the same solve. A probe writes no capacitor history, no error estimate, no step size, no chip state, no core, and no logic level. `stop_ps` is an accepted step in every run, so the last row and `observations.end_ps` are that step.

A probe whose solve stalls, is singular, or gives a quantity that is not finite is solved once more from the same accepted step, with Newton started from the end of the step after the row. If that fails too, the row is the straight line from the state the probe was solved from to the accepted step after it, read at the row's time, and the run reports `trace.row_interpolated`. After a restart that state is the restart's solve, not the row of the step before it, and its current source and switch columns read the levels and contacts after the change. The step after the row converged before any probe ran, so it is accepted either way. A probe never ends the run.

Ratings and logic come from accepted steps only. A rating's first time over and its peak are read on accepted steps. A row's logic level is the last accepted level, which is what `logic.vcd` records.

So the grid never changes the run. Two runs that differ only in `grid_ps` take the same accepted steps, stop each core at the same times, locate every chip event at the same picosecond, and write the same `logic.vcd`, `serial.csv`, `i2c.csv`, `link.csv`, `observations.json`, and `solver.*` and `rating.*` diagnostics. At every row time both have, they write the same values. Only two things depend on the grid. A grid with more rows than the trace holds is refused with `trace.too_large` before the run solves anything. A row whose probe fails twice is reported with `trace.row_interpolated`, and a grid with no row there reports nothing.

A row's probe costs one Newton solve, and two when the first fails. A grid much finer than the steps costs a solve per row, and a grid coarser than the steps costs nothing. `max_step_ps` is the only cap on a step.

The step policy is fixed. A segment starts at t = 0 and at every restart solve whose jump on a capacitor or an inductor exceeds Newton's tolerance on that row. The run compares each capacitor's current on the restart solve with its current on the step's solution. A difference `di` within `1e-6 * max(|i_step|, |i_held|) + 1e-12` A adds nothing to the sum below. That band is Newton's tolerance on the current row. The restart solve holds each capacitor at its voltage and solves the rest, so a jump inside the band is the step's companion current against the current the circuit draws at that voltage, plus what Newton leaves. Two settled solves can differ by about twice the row tolerance. The floor is that residual. The edge started no mode. The point before still moves by the raw `di`. A larger `di` starts a mode that the new drive excites. Backward Euler damps that mode, so it moves the current by at most `|di|` a step. On a mode much faster than the step the trapezoidal rule's factor is `(1 - h/2tau) / (1 + h/2tau)`, near -1, so the computed current swings from `di` to about `-di`, a move of `2|di|`, and the mode rings until the next segment start. A later edge's mode adds to it. So each capacitor sums `max_step_ps * 2|di| / 2C` over the edges that keep the segment. While every sum, this edge's included, is at most 1e-9 V, the restart solve keeps the segment, its method and its error history. Stiff stores take the trapezoidal rule on the step the segment wanted. A segment start's next step is 1 ns of backward Euler, and a kept edge does not take that step. A switch edge or a source jump that keeps the segment is still cut at the breakpoint 1 ns later, so the step after the edge is that cut, and the step after the cut is the one the segment wanted. Otherwise a segment starts, and the sums start again from zero. The next step starts from the restart solve, with the new drive in force. The point before the edge, which the next trapezoidal estimate reads, has each capacitor's current moved by its `di`. So the estimate reads the slope before the edge, not a kink of `di` over the step that landed on the edge, which would add about `h^2 * |di| / (6C * hp)`, `hp` being the step that landed. The estimate still accepts or refuses every step after the edge. A capacitor whose current was already moving can change its slope after the edge, and the estimate then refuses a long step, as it would without an edge. The sum bounds the modes that kept edges leave without a segment start, not the next step. A circuit with no capacitor and no inductor starts no segment after t = 0. A segment's first step wants 1 ns and is backward Euler. Every later step in it is trapezoidal, except on a stiff capacitor. Each step's local truncation error comes from the capacitor currents the solve already has. Backward Euler's is `h/2 * |i1 - i0| / C`. The trapezoidal rule's is `h^3 * |DD2(i)| / (6C)`, the second divided difference over the segment's last three points. The tolerance on the voltage across each capacitor is 1e-5 of its swing plus 1e-9 V. The swing is how far that voltage moves in the segment. It is the larger of two figures. One is the range of the voltage over the segment's points and the step's end. The other is the least distance to its asymptote that a step of the segment has read. A step reads `v'^2 / |v''|`, which is `h * i0^2 / (C * |i1 - i0|)` over the step, at most the circuit's span. The span is the highest net voltage minus the lowest, ground's 0 V included, and the first step reads it once at the segment's start. On an exponential `v'^2 / |v''|` is the distance to the asymptote, so an edge's first step knows the edge's swing before the voltage has moved. While 1e-5 of the swing dominates the 1 nV floor, that step is held to the change in its current, `(i1 - i0)^2 <= 2e-5 * i0^2`. Where two modes cancel the curvature at a segment's start, the first step reads past the swing, and a later step reads less. A capacitor with no current at the segment's start heads nowhere, and its swing is its own range. A stiff capacitor keeps Newton's tolerance, `1e-6 * max(|v0|, |v1|) + 1e-9` V.

A step over its tolerance on any capacitor or inductor is rejected and retried from the same accepted point. The next try is `0.9 * ratio^(-1/(p+1))` of it, between 0.25 and 0.9, where `ratio` is the worst error over its tolerance and `p` is 1 if that store took backward Euler and 2 if it took the trapezoidal rule. An accepted step grows by the same factor, at most 2, up to `max_step_ps`. When the factor is over 1 it grows by at least 1 ps. A step of 1 ps is accepted whatever its error, unless a capacitor or an inductor on the trapezoidal rule is over Newton's tolerance there. That store holds a mode faster than any step, on which the trapezoidal rule rings. It turns stiff, and the step is retried. A trapezoidal store also turns stiff at a longer step when it is over Newton's tolerance and its slope has reversed on three accepted steps running, the step being judged included. A reversal is two successive changes of opposite sign, each larger than 1e-6 times the largest of the three samples, plus 1e-12 A for a capacitor's current and 1e-9 V for an inductor's voltage. The count is per store. An accepted step that reverses adds one, and any other accepted step sets it back to zero. A segment start clears it, and a kept edge keeps it. Stiffness is judged on Newton's tolerance, not the swing's. A 20 to 43 ps mode that the swing's share would let through at 1 ps would be left ringing once the step grows past it, and a ring over Newton's tolerance is caught at the third reversal. A stiff store takes backward Euler, which damps that mode. The other stores keep the trapezoidal rule. A stiff store's backward Euler error also accumulates with one sign. The hold tries the trapezoidal rule after an accepted step of 256 ps, and also once the accepted steps of the hold add up to four times that wait. The clock also adds each accepted step while no store is held, from the segment start or from the end of the last hold. The first store on that clock can try on its next accepted step. A store that joins the hold keeps the clock. The try stays on the trapezoidal rule when each tried store's error is within the swing tolerance. A failed try puts those stores back on backward Euler, starts the clock over, and waits for a step twice as long, at least 256 ps. A ring on another store during a try sends the tried stores back to backward Euler, as a failed try does. A store already held on backward Euler keeps its wait and its clock. A try that the held time started can pass on the step that started it, when that step is shorter than the old wait. A try cut shorter by a breakpoint, a core's stop or a watch has not shown that the mode died, so the next step tries again. A segment start clears every stiff store. A kept edge ends the hold, and the stores take the trapezoidal rule on the step the segment wanted. A mode still alive after that release is caught when a later step falls to 1 ps over Newton's tolerance, or when the slope reverses three times over that tolerance. An accepted step cut short by a breakpoint or by `stop_ps` keeps the step it wanted. A rejected one does not, and its retry grows from the shorter step. With no capacitor and no inductor there is no error, so the step doubles. A step whose Newton solve fails is halved and retried from the same accepted point, down to 1 ps. A failure at 1 ps ends the run with `solver.convergence`.

An inductor is the dual of a capacitor, with its current and voltage exchanged. Its current is a branch unknown, and the voltage across its inductance comes from the node rows. Backward Euler's error on the current is `h/2 * |v1 - v0| / L` and the trapezoidal rule's is `h^3 * |DD2(v)| / (6L)`, read from the voltage across the inductance. Each step holds two limits. The current's error is under 1e-5 of the current's swing plus 1e-12 A. The voltage that error implies, `L * error / h`, is under 1e-5 of the voltage's swing plus 1e-9 V. The voltage's swing is the larger of its range over the segment and its reach, read once at the segment's start. The voltage across an inductance heads to 0 V at DC, so its reach is its distance to 0 V there, or the circuit's span if that is larger, for a coil at rest beside an edge. Without the reach, a segment's first step would hold the voltage to a share of its own move, and every edge would cut the step to a few picoseconds. The current's swing is the larger of its range and the least distance to its asymptote a step has read, `h * v0^2 / (L * |v1 - v0|)`, at most the circuit's span over the series resistance. The second limit holds the voltage where the current barely moves. A coil switched off into a diode keeps a few microamperes after the diode stops conducting, and the voltage at its end rings by tens of millivolts that the current's limit does not see. A stiff inductor keeps Newton's tolerance on its current, `1e-6 * max(|i0|, |i1|) + 1e-12` A, and the voltage limit does not apply to it. Stiffness is judged per store, so an inductor turns stiff on its own, beside capacitors that stay trapezoidal, and a part's capacitors and inductors are judged apart. At an edge an inductor sums `max_step_ps * |dv| / L`, where `dv` is the jump in the voltage across its inductance between the step's solution and the restart solve, taken as 0 when it is within `1e-6 * max(|v_step|, |v_held|) + 1e-9` V. While that sum is at most 1e-12 A, and every capacitor's at most 1e-9 V, the segment goes on. Its point before is not moved, because its voltage is a difference of node rows that other parts share.

## Chip events

A chip reads its inputs through watches. A watch is a sum of node voltages times fixed coefficients, minus an offset, and it reads high only above 0. The NE555's are TRIG minus its lower ladder node, THRES minus CONT, and RESET minus GND minus `reset_threshold`. A watch can have hysteresis, with one sum that turns it high and another that turns it low. A relay is a passive chip with two such watches, one for each sign of its coil current. Each reads the current as the voltage across the coil resistance over that resistance, since that resistance carries the inductor's current. It turns high when the current is above `pull_in_current`, and low when it is at `drop_out_current` or below. The contact is closed while either watch is high.

After each accepted step the run reads every watch on the step's solution. If any reads differently than at the last accepted point, the run locates the first picosecond where one does. Each probe re-solves the same step, from the same point with the same method, shortened to the probe time. The probe follows regula falsi on the earliest watch to flip, rounded to a whole picosecond inside the bracket. It turns to bisection once one end has moved twice in a row, and it stops at a 1 ps bracket. The located point is the later end, the first solved picosecond where a watch reads flipped, and the step lands there. A regula falsi probe whose solve stalls is replaced by the bracket's midpoint. A midpoint that stalls ends the run with `solver.convergence`, and a singular probe ends it with `solver.singular`.

A watch cuts the step, and only a stamp change takes a restart solve. At the located point each chip reacts to its levels. If a chip's new state stamps differently, or a switch edge or source jump falls there, the run takes a restart solve at that point the way it does at a switch edge. The step's solution there holds the chips' old states, and so does a row at that time. The held solve after it is never a row. A flip that changes no drive still gets its edge at its picosecond, and takes no restart solve. The segment, its method and its error history continue across it, as they do across a restart solve that moves no capacitor. In `examples/ne555-led`, THRES drops back under CONT 1 ps after the latch resets, which is such a flip.

Every watch that flips at one picosecond, on every chip, reacts at once against the levels before it, chips in part order. The run then reads the levels on the solve with the new states and reacts again, up to 8 solves. Chips that still change after the 8th are `chip.unsettled`. The logic levels recorded at the point are the ones the chips reacted to. A watch that flipped on the step keeps its flipped level there, and every other watch takes its level on the solve with the new states. When that solve is back across a flipped watch's threshold, the return is recorded at the next accepted step. A monostable with THRES on DISCH does this, since the discharge pulls THRES back down through the hold. Every located event is at a later whole picosecond, so events cannot pile up at one time. An event located at `stop_ps`, or in the step that ends there, is recorded. No restart runs at `stop_ps`.

The operating point at t = 0 settles each chip the same way. It starts from the chip's power-on state, the NE555's latch reset or a relay's contact open, and reacts to the levels it reads. Only the solve that converges at the nominal gmin and source scale is read, never an intermediate rung of the ladder.

Watches are read at accepted steps. A signal that crosses and crosses back inside one step is missed. The error control keeps steps short where a signal curves, and `max_step_ps` caps them. `grid_ps` does not.

Every chip kind keeps four rules. A watch reads node voltages, never a branch current. A passive chip's state holds only what its stamp reads, so every change of state takes a restart solve. An active chip keeps the rest in its core, and only a change of its drive image takes one. A chip's DC paths are the union over its states, so a path that some state opens, such as a high-impedance output, still counts for `net.floating`. Passive chips have no propagation delay, so two that flip each other at one time can have no states they agree on. Two NE555s, each OUT on the other's TRIG, reset together, then both falling OUTs set both latches while both THRES are still high. That is `chip.unsettled`. A relay's hysteresis keeps its contact from flipping back at the time it moves, because the restart holds the coil current within 1 fs times the coil voltage over the inductance. A relay whose contact latches its own coil therefore settles. When its `nc` feeds its own coil and the coil is powered at t = 0, it does not. Open, the coil current is above pull-in. Closed, it is below drop-out. The operating point reports `chip.unsettled`. When the coil is powered later, it holds its current across each move and the contact buzzes.

## Active chips

An active chip runs a core on the run's clock. The NE555 and the relay are passive. The ATmega328P, kind `atmega328p`, is active, and [avr.md](avr.md) describes its adapter. The SPI replay, kind `spi_replay`, is active too, and its core sends its transactions as records, as [Records](#records) says. So is the ST77916 panel, kind `st77916`, whose core has no clock, never stops, and takes records. So is the CST816, kind `cst816`, whose core has no clock. It is an I2C target, as [I2C targets](#i2c-targets) says. So is the pulse replay, kind `pulses_replay`, whose core has no clock and sends its trains as `Pulses` records. So is a `ws2812` chain, whose core has no clock, watches `DIN`, and takes `Pulses` on that net. So is the ESP32-S3, kind `esp32s3`, whose core runs on its 40 MHz crystal, and [esp32s3.md](esp32s3.md) describes it. Its console sends bytes as a USART does. A fault in its core, such as a watchdog that runs out, ends the run with the fault's error at the stop that reports it. The run builds one active chip per such part, in part order. An active chip's stamp reads only its drive image, one drive per output pin, and its fixed leakages. A pin drives high through its high stage from the chip's own VCC pin, drives low through its low stage into the chip's own GND pin, pulls up through its pull-up resistance from the chip's own VCC pin, or is off. A stage is a drop from its rail through a resistance. The drop is a source, so source stepping scales it. A pull-up is a resistance alone, so it does not scale. An output with a leakage resistance stamps it from the pin to the chip's own VCC pin and again to its GND pin, in every drive state, off included. A leakage is a resistance alone, so it does not scale either. The core holds everything else, and the run never compares the core.

Before each step the run asks each core where it next stops. A core stops at the first time its image may change, at each time it samples a voltage, and at the end of each record it sends. The step lands there. Every step therefore solves with the images in force at its start, and no accepted step is solved with a drive the core has already changed. The step's solution at a stop holds the old image, as a row there does. A sample is read on that solution, before any change at that time. If the image changed, the run takes a restart solve at the stop the way it does at a switch edge. A stop that leaves the image alone lands without one. A stop at `stop_ps` lands, and no restart runs.

A core runs ahead of the accepted time on a copy. The copy is kept when a step lands on its stop, or on a flip of one of its watches. A flip located before the copy's time drops the copy, and the core runs again from where it was last kept, to the flip. A session also keeps every core at each step that lands within one `max_step_ps` of the time the call asked for, unless the call asked for `stop_ps`. A core that does not stop runs its copy to the end of each step, and that copy is kept as it is. A retry shorter than a rejected try lands before the copy, and then the core runs again from where it was last kept to the step. So when a call that did not ask for `stop_ps` leaves the session running, its last step keeps every core, and an input after any number of such calls runs a core again over at most one `max_step_ps`. Nothing kept is ever undone. A kept copy delivers each byte its USARTs sent whose stop bit ended by its time and each TWI phase by its count, and drops its SPI bytes, which no output reads yet. A kept copy also stages each record it sent that ended by its time. An active chip takes a flip at the flip's picosecond, and a flip on a restart's held solve at the restart's time. What a flip or a sample causes comes at least 1 ps later. So an active chip never reacts at the time it reads, takes no part in the settle at one time, and is never `chip.unsettled`. An active chip's logic signals are its watches' levels, recorded as a passive chip's are. A flip is recorded at its picosecond, and a flip only the restart's held solve shows at the restart's time. So a pin the core drives reads the level its own drive makes at the drive's stop. When the held solve is back across a flip the step found, the core takes both at that picosecond, and the return is recorded at the next accepted step, as for a passive chip. Recorded at one picosecond, the pair would be dropped as a zero-time glitch, and a `RESET` or brown-out pulse the core did take would not show. No restart runs at `stop_ps`, so a drive change or a source jump there is not in the run's logic signals.

Every core keeps one contract. A run up to a limit stops at its first stop after where it last reached, or reaches the limit. A run split at any limit is the same run. A clocked core counts time in whole cycles. A clock of `f` Hz runs to cycle `floor(limit_ps * f / 1e12)` for a limit of `limit_ps`, and a stop at cycle `c` is at `ceil(c * 1e12 / f)` ps. Each clock domain counts its cycles from its last frequency change, and power-on is the first. The run checks that each stop falls after where the core reached, and that no sample, input, record or touch changes the image. A core that breaks the contract is an engine bug, and the run panics.

## I2C targets

A `cst816` is an I2C target at address 0x15. `SDA` and `INT` are open drain into the chip's own `GND`. `SCL` is an input. The part does not hold `SCL`, so a target that clock-stretches is not modelled. It drives `SDA` low to acknowledge and to send a 0, and it releases `SDA` otherwise. It answers its own address and does not answer a general call. The model answers while idle, including on the first transfer after `RST` rises. Auto-sleep, and the time a part needs to start after reset, are not modelled.

A START is `SDA` falling while `SCL` is high. A STOP is `SDA` rising while `SCL` is high. The target samples a bit on its own `SCL` rise. It moves `SDA` 300 ns after the `SCL` fall that asked for the new level. That 300 ns hold is this model's choice. UM10204 Rev. 7.0 puts the bus timing in Table 11. `tHD;DAT` for an I2C device is minimum 0. Note [2] on that row says `SCL` must cross 0.3 VDD before `SDA` changes. The 300 ns figure in that table is `tf`. `tSU;DAT` is 250 ns, 100 ns and 50 ns. Fast-mode Plus `tVD;DAT` max is 0.45 us. The hold clears the 0.3 VDD fall note with margin, and it stays inside `tVD;DAT` for every mode in the table. The target needs `SCL` low for more than 300 ns. A 20 MHz TWI with `TWBR` 0 holds `SCL` low for 450 ns. That low is shorter than Fast-mode's minimum `tLOW`, and the engine still handles it.

When a move comes due and `SCL` is already high, the target leaves `SDA` where it is, goes idle, and waits for the next START. It reports `chip.protocol` once, rule `sda_while_scl_high`, at the first time. A release it refused waits for the next `SCL` fall, so it does not keep holding the bus, and it does not drive a later byte of the transfer it left. A part in reset lets `SDA` go after the same 300 ns, whatever `SCL` reads, and that release is not the rule.

The engine calls the target in this order.

1. The eighth `SCL` rise of an address calls `matches`. A match calls `start_read` or `start_write`. Another device's address calls neither.
2. The next `SCL` fall drives the address ACK when this target matched, and leaves `SDA` released when it did not.
3. The `SCL` rise of that ACK calls `on_ack` with the time, `AckKind::Address`, and true. Another device's address does not call `on_ack`.
4. The next `SCL` fall calls `read` with the time when the transfer is a read, and releases `SDA` when the transfer is a write.
5. The eighth `SCL` rise of a written byte calls `write` with the time and the byte. `write` returns whether to ACK.
6. The next `SCL` fall drives that level.
7. The `SCL` rise of that ACK calls `on_ack` again, with `AckKind::Byte` and whether this target accepted the byte. A byte this target NACKed calls it with false. Neither call runs when a refused drive kept the level off the wire.
8. During a read, the `SCL` rise of the master's ACK or NACK calls `on_read_ack` with the time and whether the master asked for another byte. A target that samples its pins for the next byte does it here. The following `SCL` fall calls `read` for that byte when the master ACKed. A NACK ends the read.
9. A STOP calls `on_stop` with the time. A repeated START does not. The first sample after power-up or after leaving reset does not. `RST` low calls `reset` and does not call `on_stop`.

The first data byte of a write sets the register pointer. Each later write byte, and each read byte, uses that pointer and then advances it by one, wrapping at 256. [netlist.md](netlist.md#cst816) has the registers.

A touch is a breakpoint on the part. The drive image is unchanged at the touch's time. `INT` falls 1 ps later and stays low for 100 us, then releases. That width is a guess. A second touch during the pulse extends the one release. A touch while `RST` reads low is consumed and pulses nothing. `RST` going low restores the register file on that edge. `INT` releases 1 ps later. `SDA` releases 300 ns later, whatever `SCL` reads, on the same hold as any other `SDA` change.

## Records

A record is a transaction one active chip sends another. It holds its sender, its start, its end, and a sequence number that counts the sender's records from 0. A `Spi` record holds its phases, its data, the lanes the data ran on, the clock, the nets of the sender's `CS`, `SCLK` and data pins, and whether `CS` is held at its end. A `Pulses` record holds an idle level, timed levels, and the net of the sender's pin, and a `Duty` record a duty, a resolution, a frequency and the net of the sender's pin. At this version the SPI replay sends `Spi` records, the pulse replay sends `Pulses` records, the ST77916 takes `Spi` records and `Duty` records, and a `ws2812` takes `Pulses` records on its `DIN` net. No part sends a `Duty` record yet.

A record's end is a stop of its sender's core, so a step lands there. A record changes no image, on its sender or on a receiver, so a stop for a record alone lands without a restart. A chip takes a `Spi` record when its own `CS` and `SCLK` pins are on the record's nets, a `Duty` record when its own backlight pin, an ST77916's `BLK`, is on the record's net, and a `Pulses` record when its own `DIN` pin is on the record's net.

A landing runs in two passes. The first only reads. Each sender whose core stopped at the landing's time holds the records that end there, and each record goes to every chip that takes it. The second pass is the only one that runs a core again or keeps one. It gives each core its sample, its flips and its records, and keeps it. A receiver takes the records of one landing in the order they ended, then by sender name, then in the order its sender sent them. So a receiver listed before its sender still takes the record at its end, and part order changes nothing a receiver takes.

The copy kept at a record's end stages it, with every record its sender sent that ended by then. Each chip keeps how far it has staged its records, and only a kept copy moves that, so a record is staged once, across rewinds, restarts and inputs. A core run again from where it was last kept makes only records after that time.

A display part makes a row of `display.csv` when it closes a pixel write. It closes one only on a record it takes, so a row's time is the landing's, and the copy kept there stages the row. A reset on `RST` drops an open write with no row, so a flip makes no row. Rows are staged once, as records are, and a recorded session keeps them in the order they closed, then by part name, then in the order each part closed them. A display part keeps its frame memory in bands of 8 rows, shared between its kept core and its copy. A write copies only the band it lands in, and keeping a copy replaces only the bands the copy wrote, so a commit costs the bands a run touched, not the whole frame.

A `ws2812` takes the next 24 bits for each LED and discards the bits past the last LED. It has no `DOUT` pin. Green is the first byte, then red, then blue, most significant bit first. A bit is a 0 or a 1 by its high alone, T0H 220 ns to 380 ns or T1H 580 ns to 1 µs from the Worldsemi WS2812E-1313 datasheet, and any low up to 280 µs ends it. A high in neither window is dropped, and the first one is `chip.protocol` rule `bit`. A low longer than 280 µs, RES, latches, and the row's time is the fall plus 280 µs plus 1 ps. Exactly 280 µs is not a reset. [netlist.md](netlist.md#ws2812) gives each number's page. The latch is not a drive change, so it takes no restart.

A `Pulses` record runs its levels from its start. After the last level the line is back at the record's idle level. A line held at its idle level, from power-on or after a record, starts its next high at the next record's start, so an idle-high line does not stretch the first high. A held line under a record that opens on a low falls at the record's start, and that fall ends no bit. A high that a record ends on is a bit, and it keeps its own start when the next record opens with its low. A reset that falls inside a pulse latches there, before the record ends. A low that runs past a record's end arms a stop at its reset, as a fall on the pin does. A record lands only at its end, so the next record may have started before that stop. When it has, the chain goes back to where it was before that latch and runs the record from its start, so a frame split across records latches once, as the whole frame does. Until that record lands, `leds` shows the early latch. When the run ends inside a record, the chain takes the levels the record sent up to the end, with the same going back. A level that starts exactly at the end is taken, as a landing there takes the return to idle, and a record that starts exactly at the end counts as sent. So the ended session and its run folder show the line as it was at the end, whether the frame was one record or two.

The rows live on the core, shared between the kept core and its copy, so keeping a copy copies none of them. A recorded session writes them at the end, ordered by time, then by part name.

## Sessions

A session is a transient run held between calls. It opens on a netlist whose analysis is `transient`, and solves the operating point at t = 0 and the step after it. It keeps no trace. An `op` analysis has no time to advance and opens no session. A circuit too large for the solver opens none, with `solver.too_large`. Neither does an operating point at t = 0 that fails to settle or gives a quantity that is not finite, and the diagnostic is the one a run reports. A grid with more rows than a trace holds still opens a session.

`diagnostics()` returns the warnings the session has found, in `diagnostics.json`'s schema and order. Once the session ends, they are the warnings its `diagnostics.json` lists. While it runs, a `Pulses` record still in flight is not yet counted, as with `leds`. A bit that record has sent outside both windows shows once the record lands or the session ends. The list holds each static warning, such as `net.floating`, with `t_ps` null. It also holds one `rating.current` or `rating.power` for each rated quantity that has gone over, and each `chip.protocol` and `chip.link_miswired` a kept core has reported. `t_ps` is the first accepted step over that rating. `peak` and `peak_ps` run through the last accepted step. `over_ps` runs through that step, not to `now`. A step that is over adds its time when the next step is accepted, so a quantity first over on the last accepted step reads 0. The fault that ends a run is `fault`, and it is not in this list. `trace.row_interpolated` is not in the list either. That warning names a grid row whose probe failed twice, and only a recorded trace has those rows. [diagnostics.md](diagnostics.md) names every warning a folder can hold.

`start` fills the floating-net warnings once, when the session opens. Ratings are sampled on each accepted step, the operating point at t = 0 included. An open session and a recorded session both sample them. A call that accepts no new step leaves the list unchanged. After an input, a finding already present stays, and its `t_ps` stays. The peak and the time over can still grow when a later accepted step goes further over.

`advance_to` takes a time and accepts every step that lands at or before it. `stop_ps` caps the time. A running session then stands at that time, which is `now`. A time at or before `now` does nothing, so `now` never moves back. The step after the last accepted one is already solved, and it waits until a call reaches its time. A call whose time falls inside that step leaves it solved and waiting. The call never cuts the step at its time, which would make that time a breakpoint and let the call times move the steps. It never accepts the step early either, which would put `now` at a time no call asked for. So a session advanced to `stop_ps` in any number of calls takes the steps of one call. `sim run` is one such call, on a session that records every accepted step.

A probe reads every net voltage and every part's quantities at `now` in one call. A bipolar transistor reports `ib`, `ic` and `p`. An `nmos` reports `id` and `p`. A chip and a potentiometer have none, and every other part reports `i`, `v` and `p`. On an accepted step it is that step's row, read before any restart there, and it solves nothing. Between steps it is solved from the last accepted step and retried the way a row is, as [Rows](#rows) says. So at a row time the probe is the trace's row, bit for bit. When both its solves fail, the probe is the straight line and says so. `signals` lists each chip signal, chips in part order. `levels` reads them all at `now` in that order, and `level` reads one, both with no solve. A level is the last accepted level, which is what `logic.vcd` holds at `now`. A probe and a level change nothing.

The same call carries the integral of each analog column from t = 0 to the probe's time, in `refs` order. The unit is the column's unit times picoseconds. Each accepted step adds a trapezoid of that column. The left end is the restart row when the step restarted, and the accepted row otherwise. The right end is the landing row. The addition follows the recorder keeping the span, so a refused step is left out. It precedes the restart solve, so a failed restart is included, and the session ends on that landing row. Between steps the probe adds one trapezoid from the same left end to its own values. That piece is not stored. A caller subtracts two readings and divides by the time between them. With no input applied between the calls, the reading at one time has the same bits for every split of the calls that reached it. An input can change a second reading at that same time, as it can change the probe values. An input can change a reading at `now` only in three cases. The re-solve accepts a step that ends before `now`. A store, a capacitor or an inductor, is stiff on the old held step and not on the new one, or on the new one and not the old. Or the probe's first solve, started from the accepted step, fails, so it solves again with the held step's landing as its starting guess. The integrals are plain `f64` values, and a huge finite column can overflow one to infinity. The run folder does not store these integrals.

The session keeps each change of a chip signal on an accepted step. Those are the changes `logic.vcd` holds, in signal order within one time. The levels at t = 0 are not changes. `edges_since` takes a cursor, `EdgeCursor::default()` at first, and returns the changes after the ones it has read and the cursor past them. It solves nothing and changes nothing. A cursor converts to and from a `u64`, the count of changes since t = 0. A cursor past the end reads nothing and stays. `edge_cursor` is the cursor past every change so far. `levels` read at the same time is the t = 0 levels with every earlier change applied. An unrecorded session keeps that log itself. `forget_edges` drains the changes before a cursor, so the log holds only what the caller has not forgotten. A recorded session reads the recorder's change list, the one `logic.vcd` is written from, and keeps no second copy. `forget_edges` on a recorded session only raises the read floor. The changes stay, so `logic.vcd` is unchanged. Cursors keep counting from the first change. `edges_since` returns `None` for a cursor before the forgotten changes. The log stops at the last accepted step, as a level does, and there is no change between that step and `now`. An input can expose a change before a reader's last time, as it can change a probe. A `Change` is `size_of::<Change>()` bytes. That is 24 bytes when `usize` is 8 bytes, and 16 bytes when `usize` is 4 bytes. While a recorded session runs, its edge log and its serial log both hold every entry through the latest step `advance_to` has accepted. At `stop_ps`, and on a fault other than a failed restart, both hold every entry through `now`. A failed restart sets `now` to its landing. The edge log holds no change at that landing. The serial log holds every byte whose stop bit ended by that landing. An unrecorded session keeps those same entries. `forget_edges` and `forget_serial` drain what the caller has forgotten.

`usarts` lists each USART, chips in part order, then each chip's in datasheet order, as the part's name and the USART's, such as `USART0`. Each call ends by delivering every byte whose stop bit ended at or before the last accepted step, from the copies each core runs ahead on, and appends them to the session's serial log. A byte holds its start-bit time, the index of its USART in `usarts`, and its value. The start-bit time is the first picosecond at or after the oscillator cycle the start bit began, which is where the core stops for the `TXD` drive change. The log stops at the accepted step, as a level does, because the span from there to `now` is not settled. An input solves the held step again, and the new steps can locate a watch flip before `now` that the old step did not show. A byte known only from that span could then be one the run never sends. So a byte reaches the log once a step lands at or after the end of its stop bit, at most one step after it, and at `stop_ps` or a fault the log holds every byte that ended by `now`. The log is in the order stop bits ended, then by part name, then by the USART's index in `usarts`, then in the order its chip sent them, because a byte is known only once its stop bit ends. Parse sorts parts by name, so that index order is the part order a parsed netlist has. On one USART that is also the order bytes started. With two USARTs at different bauds, a byte can start before the one ahead of it. `serial_since` takes a cursor, `SerialCursor::default()` at first, and returns the bytes after the ones it has read and the cursor past them. It solves nothing and changes nothing. A cursor converts to and from a `u64`, the count of bytes read, so it crosses a boundary that has no Rust types. A cursor past the end, from another session or a count the caller made, reads nothing and stays. Each byte is in the log once, across rewinds, restarts and inputs, so a session advanced to `stop_ps` in any number of calls holds the bytes of one call. A frame still on the wire at `stop_ps` or at a fault is not in the log. While the session runs, a session on its export, advanced to the export's stop, holds the session's bytes first, then the ones up to that stop. Once the session has ended, they are the same bytes, because a session on the export takes the steps the session took to the same end, `stop_ps` or the same fault. The step limit is the same for every run and is not in the document, so a step-limit fault is the same fault there. Each byte is `size_of::<SerialByte>()` bytes, which is 16. An unrecorded session drains the bytes before a cursor in `forget_serial`, so the log holds only what the caller has not forgotten. A recorded session keeps every delivered byte in that one list. `forget_serial` only raises the read floor, and `finish` still writes every byte to `serial.csv`. Cursors keep counting from the first byte. `serial_since` returns `None` for a cursor before the forgotten bytes, and a cursor past the end still reads nothing and stays. `sim run` writes its session's log as `traces/serial.csv`. That is the log at `stop_ps`, or after a fault the log at the fault's `now`.

A recorded session also keeps each TWI phase that ended at or before the last accepted step, delivered on the same calls as the bytes. One delivery is in the order phases ended, then in part order. A phase that ends on an input can end within one CPU cycle before a delivery already made, and that row is then written after a row that ended later. `sim run` writes them as `traces/i2c.csv`. A phase can end on an input, such as a STOP on the SDA rise the TWI reads, at a time a delivery has already passed. So each core numbers its phases, the count carries in its copies, and a phase is delivered once by its number, across rewinds, restarts and inputs. An unrecorded session drops the phases.

A recorded session also keeps each record that ended at or before the last accepted step, delivered on the same calls as the bytes. They are in the order records ended, then by sender name, then in the order each sender sent them. `sim run` writes them as `traces/link.csv` when a chip in the netlist can send records. An unrecorded session drops the records.

`display` takes a part name and returns its display part as the last accepted step left it, which is its state at `now`, or `None` when the part is no display part. Its `panel` is the state an `observations.json` `displays` entry holds without the image, and its `image` is the frame memory `traces/display-<part>.rgb565` holds. A read changes nothing. `sim run` writes the rows of a recorded session as `traces/display.csv`, and each display part's image at the end as its file. An unrecorded session drops the rows.

`leds` takes a part name and returns its chain as the last accepted step left it, the `observations.json` `leds` entry, or `None` when the part is no chain. A read changes nothing. `sim run` writes the latches of a recorded session as `traces/leds.csv`. The colours stay on the core, so an unrecorded session can still be read.

A fault is any error that ends a run after t = 0, the step limit included. A fault other than a failed restart ends the session at its last accepted step, and `now` stays there. The session solves each step as soon as it accepts the one before it. So the call that accepts the last good step returns the fault, even when its time is before the step that failed, and a session can end as it opens. A failed restart sets `now` to its landing. Every later call returns the same fault. A session that reaches `stop_ps` ends with no fault. Each call returns whether the session is running, stopped or faulted, and `fault` reads the fault.

`apply` takes an input: a part, a time and a setting. A setting is a switch's contact, a level in that field's unit, the bytes a `serial` source sends, or a point on a `cst816`. A voltage source reads the level as `voltage`, in volts. A current source reads it as `current`, in amperes. A potentiometer reads it as `position`, the fraction of its resistance from `a` to the wiper. A serial send is 8N1 frames at the baud the input states. The input lands at the first time from the one it asks for that is at or after `now`, after the accepted step, at or after that step's reach, and allowed after the part's last event. With no event that is 1 ps. After a level it is 2 ns later. After a switch event it is the event's bounce window and 2 ns later, so an input too close to the last one is moved, not refused. A serial send lands at the latest of that time and the end of the source's last event. The start bit is at the landing. A send asked for while those frames are still on the wire starts when the line returns to idle. The 2 ns gap a level uses does not apply to it. A touch lands at the latest of the time a contact uses and 1 ps, or 2000 ps after the part's last touch. A point equal to the last one still lands. The drive image does not change at that time. One `apply` appends one event and solves the held step once, however many bytes the send holds. The event goes after the part's last one. A switch event takes the bounce parse draws for an event at that index, so no earlier edge or draw moves. The held step is solved again from the accepted step with the new edge among the breakpoints, and any step that then lands by `now` is accepted. A level or a serial start bit is a jump, so that solve restarts, as it does at a switch edge. A part keeps one pending breakpoint however many inputs it takes. `apply` returns the time the input landed and the session's status after the re-solve. A failed restart on that re-solve ends the session at the landing. Any other fault on the re-solve ends it at the accepted step. An input whose re-solve faults stays applied, and the export holds the event when the session has a document. It refuses an input on a session that has ended, on a name no part has, on a resistor, a capacitor, an inductor, a relay, a junction, an `npn`, a `pnp`, an `nmos`, a chip other than a `cst816`, or a `step` or `pulse` source, on a contact for a source or a potentiometer, on a level for a switch, on a position outside (0, 1) or a position whose leg breaks the resistance rules, on a contact or a level that equals the value after the part's last event, a pending event included, and on one that would land at or after `stop_ps`. A serial send on a resistor, a capacitor, an inductor, a relay, a junction, an `npn`, a `pnp`, an `nmos`, a chip, or a `step` or `pulse` source is refused the same way. A serial send on a switch, a potentiometer, or a plain or `steps` source is refused, and so is a contact or a level on a `serial` source. A touch on a resistor, a capacitor, an inductor, a relay, a junction, a chip other than a `cst816`, or a `step` or `pulse` source is refused. A touch on a switch, a potentiometer, or a source is refused. A coordinate outside 0 to 4095 is refused. A serial send with no bytes is refused. A plain source does not become a `serial` source. A send whose frames would not end inside a time the engine can store is refused, as one that would land at or after `stop_ps` is. A refused input changes nothing.

The reach is the furthest time any attempt toward an accepted step reached. A step tries its longest first. A rejected or stalled try is shortened, a stiffened one is tried again, and a watch flip is bracketed inside the try that crossed it. So the accepted step can end before an attempt did, and before `now`. A batch run has the input's edge from t = 0, and that edge cuts every attempt past it. An edge before the reach would cut an attempt the session made, and the batch could accept a step other than the one the session kept. At or after the reach the batch makes the session's attempts, and the held step is solved the way the batch solves it. In most runs the reach is the accepted step, and an input lands at `now`, or 1 ps after it when `now` is the accepted step. After a segment start whose first step was rejected it is up to 1 ns later, and after a watch cut up to one `max_step_ps` later.

`open_document` opens a session on netlist.json bytes and keeps the document. `export` writes it back with each applied input in its part. A switch event is appended to `events`. A plain `voltage`, `current` or `position` becomes a `steps` waveform whose `initial` is that value object and whose `events` hold the applied levels. A `steps` field gains those levels on its `events`. A `serial` field gains one event per applied send, with `t_ps`, `baud`, `frame` and `bytes`. A `cst816` gains each applied point on `touches`, with `t_ps`, `x`, `y` and `down`. A level event carries the SI number, the field's unit and `"source": "user"`. A part with no applied input is left as it was. A session `open` made from a netlist has no document to export. The export is reprinted, with keys sorted and numbers rewritten, and its parts and nets keep their order. While the session runs, the export's `stop_ps` is the held step's reach, raised to `max_step_ps` and capped at the document's `stop_ps`, and `grid_ps` is cut to it. That is at most one `max_step_ps` past `now`. A batch run of the export takes every step the session accepted and the step it holds, bit for bit, then takes its own steps to its stop. A `stop_ps` at `now` would be a breakpoint at `now`, which splits the run where an input there would. Once the session has ended the export keeps the document's `stop_ps`, and a batch run of it takes every step the session took, to the same fault.

`record_document` opens a recorded session on netlist.json bytes. It keeps the trace and the logic history `sim run` keeps. Every session keeps the floating-net warnings and the rating findings, an open session included. It refuses what `open_document` refuses, and also a grid with more rows than a trace holds, with `trace.too_large`, where `sim run` writes a folder that reports it. `simulate` opens its transient run the same way. `finish` consumes a recorded session, advances it to `stop_ps`, and returns the run folder `sim run` writes for the netlist the session ran, stamped with the `created_at` the caller passes. With no input applied that netlist is the bytes `record_document` read, as `sim run` writes its input unchanged, and an export would reprint them. Once an input is applied it is the export of the ended session, whose `stop_ps` is the document's, and `sim run` on that export writes the same folder byte for byte. An export taken while the session runs has a shorter `stop_ps`, so it is not that netlist. A faulted session finishes to the folder that reports its fault. A session `open` or `open_document` made records nothing, and `finish` returns no folder for it.

## Junctions

Each solve is Newton's method. A diode or LED holds one junction voltage between iterations, a zener holds two, and a transistor holds two, `vbe` and `vbc`. Each is limited with SPICE's `pnjlim` against its own `vcrit`, which comes from the diode's `emission_coefficient`, a zener's forward `emission_coefficient` and its derived breakdown junction, or a transistor's `forward_emission` for `vbe` and its `reverse_emission` for `vbc`. A forward step past `vcrit` by more than `2 * n*VT` is cut to a logarithmic step, and a reverse step is floored. Each evaluation clamps the exponent at 700 and the slope at 1e100. A diode starts at its `vcrit`. A zener starts each junction at that junction's `vcrit`. A transistor starts with `vbe` at its `vcrit` and `vbc` at 0. An iteration in which any junction was limited or clamped has not converged. Neither has one in which a linearised current misses its device equation at the solved voltages by more than 1e-6 of its size plus 1e-12 A. A transistor checks both `ic` and `ib`. A zener checks each junction current. A converged iterate passes through the limiter bit for bit. A diode or an LED puts `GMIN`, 1e-12 S, across its junction and puts no node gmin on its internal node. A zener puts `GMIN` across each of its two junctions and puts no node gmin on either internal node. A transistor's `GMIN`, 1e-12 S across each junction, is in its equations, and its internal nodes take no node gmin either. Node gmin stays on every external net. An `nmos` holds `vgs` and `vds` and passes each through with no limiter. It starts at 0 and 0. Its convergence check is the drain current. Its `GMIN`, 1e-12 S from drain to source, is in that current. It has no internal node. A linear regulator reads its last branch current and its last input voltage. It has no junction, so it takes no `pnjlim` and no limiting. Its convergence check is the output voltage.

## Accuracy

`grid_ps` sets how dense the trace is and nothing else. `max_step_ps` caps the step. Neither sets the tolerance. Each trapezoidal step above the 1 ps floor holds that capacitor's local truncation error under 1e-5 of its own swing plus 1 nV. The step after every edge starts from the edge's restart solve, and an edge whose jump exceeds Newton's tolerance on a capacitor or an inductor starts a segment there. That bound is per step and per capacitor. The error the steps accumulate is measured separately. On one mode it stays under 1e-4 of the swing. On an exponential, the trapezoidal rule's error over what is left of the swing grows by `x^3 / 12` a step, `x` being the step over the time constant. A step that rides 1e-5 of the whole swing has `x = 0.9 * (12e-5 * e^(t / tau))^(1/3)`. Its error peaks about 1.6 time constants after the edge at `0.039 * (12e-5)^(2/3)`, 9.5e-5 of the swing. A step never reads more than the whole swing, so this bounds one exponential. Measured from 100 ps to 1 ms, the worst at any step is 5.8e-5 to 7.2e-5. A step capped at `h` by `max_step_ps` has `x = h / tau`, and its error peaks one time constant after the edge at `x^2 / (12e)` of the swing. A swing under 100 uV is held to the 1 nV floor instead. A 100 uV edge, where 1e-5 of the swing equals that floor, measures 1.2e-4 of its swing. The tolerance holds a small swing on a large level to its own size. A 10.6 mV sag on a 5 V rail is 7.1e-5 of the sag off. The RC example's rows are 1.2e-6 V from its closed form, 4.9e-7 of the 2.5 V span. Its 20 us `max_step_ps` caps the 5 ms charge at 0.004 of the time constant, and `x^2 / (12e)` of the span is that miss. A probed row is one more step from the last accepted point, no longer than the step that accepts after it, so it carries the error of the steps around it. `examples/v-pulse-rc` has a 1 us grid and its `max_step_ps` is its 100 us time constant, so the tolerance alone sets its steps. Its rows are 7.1e-5 V from the closed form, 8.2e-5 of the 0.87 V span, under the 9.5e-5 of one exponential. A 10 ns discharge after an edge stays under 1e-4. Where modes couple, the accumulated error can exceed the one-mode figure, because a capacitor carries part of a neighbour's error. In 460 three-capacitor ladders from random states, the worst capacitor was 1.2e-4 of its own swing off. Engine 0.1.0 held every step to `1e-6 * max(|v0|, |v1|) + 1e-9` V, and on those ladders its worst capacitor was 8.8e-4 of its own swing off. A threshold crossing moves by the error over the slope there, up to `1e-4 * tau * swing / left`, `left` being what is left of the swing at the threshold. A located chip event carries that shift. The driven-CONT astable's period moves by 1.5e-4. The OUT edges of `examples/ne555-led` move by 5.49e-6 of their time at the largest. The tolerance change accounts for 5.39e-6 of that move. The later probe-row change accounts for the rest.

A step at the floor is accepted over its tolerance, so a time constant under about 43 ps is not held to it. A discharge after an edge measures this, as its worst error over its swing:

| Time constant | Worst error |
| --- | --- |
| 1 ps | 0.13 |
| 10 ps | 1.8e-2 |
| 30 ps | 6.0e-3 |
| 50 ps | 1.7e-4 |
| 100 ps | 6.6e-5 |
| 300 ps | 7.2e-5 |
| 1 ns and up | 6.9e-5 |

Under about 43 ps the capacitor turns stiff, and the error is about 0.18 times 1 ps over the time constant. Near 50 ps the steps sit at the floor. From 100 ps up the capacitor stays trapezoidal, and the error is what its steps accumulate, about 7e-5. The restart's hold shifts the start of the discharge by 1 fs over the time constant, as a fraction of the swing. A stiff capacitor whose voltage keeps moving after its mode dies goes back to the trapezoidal rule. 10 pF dumped through 2 ohm into 1 nF, then both charged through 1 Mohm, stays under 1e-4 of its closed form, at 6.4e-5. So does a 10 ns discharge beside a 30 ps mode switched in, at 4.8e-5. A hold that never accepts a step of 256 ps tries the trapezoidal rule once its accepted steps add up to four times 256 ps, and a failed try starts that wait over. A capacitor whose tries keep failing stays on backward Euler until a try passes or an edge ends the hold. It keeps backward Euler's error, which accumulates with one sign. It also takes more steps, since that error grows with the square of the step. A capacitor on a separate node keeps its accuracy beside it, and one that shares its slow mode shares its error. Two 10 pF coupled through 1 ohm and charged through 900 ohm, with a common mode of 18 ns, stay stiff and take about thirty times the steps of one 20 pF capacitor, which takes the swing's longer trapezoidal steps. While the common mode moves they are up to 2.3e-4 of their swing off, and 3.9e-5 after 100 ns. Halving `max_step_ps` and comparing the traces is still a check for a circuit with no closed form.

`examples/rl-step` steps 5 V across 0.505 H and 70 ohm, a 7.2 ms time constant, and back to 0 V. Its rows are at most 4.7e-7 A from the closed form, 6.6e-6 of the 71 mA swing. It accepts 405 steps. With only the current's limit it would accept 167, and be 7.1e-5 of the swing off. Of the 238 more steps, 224 fall 1 ms or more after an edge, as the current decays. The trapezoidal rule's error implies a voltage of `h^2 * |v''| / 12`. On an exponential the voltage limit holds the step to about `0.011 * tau * e^(t/2tau)`, and the current's limit to about `0.049 * tau * e^(t/3tau)`, `t` from the edge. So the voltage limit sets the step for the first nine time constants after an edge. A coil driven much faster than its time constant pays nothing for it. A 5 V pulse 10 us wide every 20 us onto 10 mH and 10 ohm takes 14013 steps over 10 ms with both limits, and the same with the current's alone. A 0.505 H, 70 ohm coil switched on at 2 ms and off at 30 ms into a diode across it is, at every row, within 0.0210 of the bound the ngspice comparison holds it to. With only the current's limit it is 10.25 times that bound on the coil's switched end at 46.2 ms, after the diode stops conducting, where the end rings from 4.94 V to 5.012 V and ngspice holds it at 4.99965 V.
