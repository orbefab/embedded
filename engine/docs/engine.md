# Engine

This page describes the engine at its `engine_version`. It is not part of the netlist or the run folder format. [netlist.md](netlist.md) says what a document means, and this page says how the engine computes it. A change here moves `engine_version`, as [versions.md](versions.md) says.

A run folder does not depend on the target the engine was built for, because no float result that reaches it is left to the platform. The `f64` methods `exp`, `ln` and `cbrt` call the platform's math library, and two platforms can round their last bits differently. The engine calls the `libm` crate for them instead, which computes each one in Rust from operations IEEE 754 rounds the same way everywhere. `sqrt` stays the `f64` method, because IEEE 754 rounds it exactly. `clippy.toml` bans the `f64` and `f32` methods whose result depends on the platform, tests included, so a test's closed form is held to the same rule. Every example's run folder is the same on x86-64 Linux and on `wasm32-unknown-unknown`, `created_at` aside. No other target was measured. A target whose `f64` arithmetic is not IEEE 754 binary64, such as 32-bit x86 without SSE2, is outside this.

## Transient steps

The run solves the operating point at t = 0, then steps to `stop_ps`. Every switch edge, every source jump, and every potentiometer move before `stop_ps` is a breakpoint, and so is every stop of an active chip's core, as [Active chips](#active-chips) says. So is 1 ns after t = 0, and 1 ns after each edge or jump, when that time is before `stop_ps`. A step lands on each breakpoint and on `stop_ps`. A row time is not a breakpoint. Only the row times are written to the trace, as [Rows](#rows) says. Every accepted step also feeds the envelope. A row closes the interval open since the previous row. A step that is not a row folds into that interval. A restart solve, a step the error control refused, and a probe locating a chip event are not samples. The accepted step the probe lands is.

At each switch edge or source jump the step's solution holds the old contact or level. Then the run solves once more at that time, with the new contacts and levels in force and every capacitor held at its voltage, the way an `initial` voltage is held at t = 0. This restart solve gives the currents just after the change. It is never a row, and the next step starts from it. Each held voltage moves by the capacitor's current times 1 fs over its capacitance. A jump at `stop_ps` gets no restart. A `steps` event or a potentiometer move is a jump under that rule.

The run reads each part's edges one at a time, in time order, so it never lists a schedule whole. A dense schedule costs steps, not rows. The row cap counts grid rows only. A run accepts at most 100000000 steps, not counting t = 0. The limit sits far above the steps a run whose rows fit needs on ordinary circuits. At this step policy a 1 MHz pin into a 10 ns load costs about 170000 steps per simulated ms. The limit allows about 590 ms of such a pin. The step policy, not this limit, bounds an MCU-rate run. Reaching the limit before `stop_ps` ends the run with `solver.step_limit` at the last accepted time.

Solver tests that need every accepted step use `simulate_steps`, behind the test-only `step-rows` feature. A `sim` build does not have it. The test-only step-rows mode can also stop mid-run with `trace.too_large`, with `t_ps` the step that did not fit, keeping its rows.

## Rows

A row is a probe, never a landing. It is solved from the last accepted step to the row time, with the method, the chip states and the drive images the next step uses, the way a probe locating a chip event is. The run never accepts it. When a step lands at a row's time anyway, on an edge, a core stop or `stop_ps`, the row is that step, which is the same solve. A probe writes no capacitor history, no error estimate, no step size, no chip state, no core, and no logic level. `stop_ps` is an accepted step in every run, so the last row and `observations.end_ps` are that step.

A probe whose solve stalls, is singular, or gives a quantity that is not finite is solved once more from the same accepted step, with Newton started from the end of the step after the row. If that fails too, the row is the straight line from the state the probe was solved from to the accepted step after it, read at the row's time, and the run reports `trace.row_interpolated`. After a restart that state is the restart's solve, not the row of the step before it, and its current source and switch columns read the levels and contacts after the change. The step after the row converged before any probe ran, so it is accepted either way. A probe never ends the run.

Ratings and logic come from accepted steps only. A rating's first time over and its peak are read on accepted steps. A row's logic level is the last accepted level, which is what `logic.vcd` records.

So the grid never changes the run. Two runs that differ only in `grid_ps` take the same accepted steps, stop each core at the same times, locate every chip event at the same picosecond, and write the same `logic.vcd`, `serial.csv`, `observations.json`, and `solver.*` and `rating.*` diagnostics. At every row time both have, they write the same values. Only two things depend on the grid. A grid with more rows than the trace holds is refused with `trace.too_large` before the run solves anything. A row whose probe fails twice is reported with `trace.row_interpolated`, and a grid with no row there reports nothing.

A row's probe costs one Newton solve, and two when the first fails. A grid much finer than the steps costs a solve per row, and a grid coarser than the steps costs nothing. `max_step_ps` is the only cap on a step.

The step policy is fixed. A segment starts at t = 0 and at every restart solve that moves a capacitor. The run compares each capacitor's current on the restart solve with its current on the step's solution. A difference `di` starts a mode that the new drive excites. Backward Euler damps that mode, so it moves the current by at most `|di|` a step. On a mode much faster than the step the trapezoidal rule's factor is `(1 - h/2tau) / (1 + h/2tau)`, near -1, so the computed current swings from `di` to about `-di`, a move of `2|di|`, and the mode rings until the next segment start. A later edge's mode adds to it. So each capacitor sums `max_step_ps * 2|di| / 2C` over the edges that keep the segment. While every sum, this edge's included, is at most 1e-9 V, the restart solve keeps the segment, its method, its error history, its stiff capacitors and the step it wanted. Otherwise a segment starts, and the sums start again from zero. The next step starts from the restart solve, with the new drive in force. The point before the edge, which the next trapezoidal estimate reads, has each capacitor's current moved by its `di`. So the estimate reads the slope before the edge, not a kink of `di` over the step that landed on the edge, which would add about `h^2 * |di| / (6C * hp)`, `hp` being the step that landed. The estimate still accepts or refuses every step after the edge. A capacitor whose current was already moving can change its slope after the edge, and the estimate then refuses a long step, as it would without an edge. The sum bounds the modes that kept edges leave without a segment start, not the next step. A capacitor at rest beside the edge keeps the segment, though the two solves give it currents that differ in their last bits. A circuit with no capacitor starts no segment after t = 0. A switch edge or a source jump that keeps the segment still has its breakpoint 1 ns later, so the step after it is cut there, and the step after that is the one the segment wanted. A segment's first step wants 1 ns and is backward Euler. Every later step in it is trapezoidal, except on a stiff capacitor. Each step's local truncation error comes from the capacitor currents the solve already has. Backward Euler's is `h/2 * |i1 - i0| / C`. The trapezoidal rule's is `h^3 * |DD2(i)| / (6C)`, the second divided difference over the segment's last three points. The tolerance on the voltage across each capacitor is 1e-5 of its swing plus 1e-9 V. The swing is how far that voltage moves in the segment. It is the larger of two figures. One is the range of the voltage over the segment's points and the step's end. The other is the least distance to its asymptote that a step of the segment has read. A step reads `v'^2 / |v''|`, which is `h * i0^2 / (C * |i1 - i0|)` over the step, at most the circuit's span. The span is the highest net voltage minus the lowest, ground's 0 V included, and the first step reads it once at the segment's start. On an exponential `v'^2 / |v''|` is the distance to the asymptote, so an edge's first step knows the edge's swing before the voltage has moved. While 1e-5 of the swing dominates the 1 nV floor, that step is held to the change in its current, `(i1 - i0)^2 <= 2e-5 * i0^2`. Where two modes cancel the curvature at a segment's start, the first step reads past the swing, and a later step reads less. A capacitor with no current at the segment's start heads nowhere, and its swing is its own range. A stiff capacitor keeps Newton's tolerance, `1e-6 * max(|v0|, |v1|) + 1e-9` V.

A step over its tolerance on any capacitor is rejected and retried from the same accepted point. The next try is `0.9 * ratio^(-1/(p+1))` of it, between 0.25 and 0.9, where `ratio` is the worst error over its tolerance and `p` is 1 if that capacitor took backward Euler and 2 if it took the trapezoidal rule. An accepted step grows by the same factor, at most 2, up to `max_step_ps`. When the factor is over 1 it grows by at least 1 ps. A step of 1 ps is accepted whatever its error, unless a capacitor on the trapezoidal rule is over Newton's tolerance there. That capacitor holds a mode faster than any step, on which the trapezoidal rule rings. It turns stiff, and the step is retried. Stiffness is judged on Newton's tolerance, not the swing's. A 20 to 43 ps mode that the swing's share would let through at 1 ps would be left ringing once the step grows past it. A stiff capacitor's backward Euler error also accumulates with one sign. A stiff capacitor takes backward Euler, which damps that mode. The other capacitors keep the trapezoidal rule. Once a stiff capacitor's accepted step reaches 256 ps, the next step tries it on the trapezoidal rule. The try holds only if each tried capacitor's error is under 1e-3 of Newton's tolerance, because what is left of the mode does not decay on the trapezoidal rule. Otherwise the step is retried with them on backward Euler, and the next try waits for a step twice as long. Only an accepted step at least as long as the one the try waited for, 256 ps or twice the failed try, ends it. A try cut shorter by a breakpoint, a core's stop or a watch has not shown that the mode died, so the next step tries again. A segment's start clears every stiff capacitor. An accepted step cut short by a breakpoint or by `stop_ps` keeps the step it wanted. A rejected one does not, and its retry grows from the shorter step. With no capacitor there is no error, so the step doubles. A step whose Newton solve fails is halved and retried from the same accepted point, down to 1 ps. A failure at 1 ps ends the run with `solver.convergence`.

## Chip events

A chip reads its inputs through watches. A watch is a sum of node voltages times fixed coefficients, minus an offset, and it reads high only above 0. The NE555's are TRIG minus its lower ladder node, THRES minus CONT, and RESET minus GND minus `reset_threshold`.

After each accepted step the run reads every watch on the step's solution. If any reads differently than at the last accepted point, the run locates the first picosecond where one does. Each probe re-solves the same step, from the same point with the same method, shortened to the probe time. The probe follows regula falsi on the earliest watch to flip, rounded to a whole picosecond inside the bracket. It turns to bisection once one end has moved twice in a row, and it stops at a 1 ps bracket. The located point is the later end, the first solved picosecond where a watch reads flipped, and the step lands there. A regula falsi probe whose solve stalls is replaced by the bracket's midpoint. A midpoint that stalls ends the run with `solver.convergence`, and a singular probe ends it with `solver.singular`.

A watch cuts the step, and only a stamp change takes a restart solve. At the located point each chip reacts to its levels. If a chip's new state stamps differently, or a switch edge or source jump falls there, the run takes a restart solve at that point the way it does at a switch edge. The step's solution there holds the chips' old states, and so does a row at that time. The held solve after it is never a row. A flip that changes no drive still gets its edge at its picosecond, and takes no restart solve. The segment, its method and its error history continue across it, as they do across a restart solve that moves no capacitor. In `examples/ne555-led`, THRES drops back under CONT 1 ps after the latch resets, which is such a flip.

Every watch that flips at one picosecond, on every chip, reacts at once against the levels before it, chips in part order. The run then reads the levels on the solve with the new states and reacts again, up to 8 solves. Chips that still change after the 8th are `chip.unsettled`. The logic levels recorded at the point are the ones the chips reacted to. A watch that flipped on the step keeps its flipped level there, and every other watch takes its level on the solve with the new states. When that solve is back across a flipped watch's threshold, the return is recorded at the next accepted step. A monostable with THRES on DISCH does this, since the discharge pulls THRES back down through the hold. Every located event is at a later whole picosecond, so events cannot pile up at one time. An event located at `stop_ps`, or in the step that ends there, is recorded. No restart runs at `stop_ps`.

The operating point at t = 0 settles each chip the same way. It starts from the chip's power-on state, the NE555's latch reset, and reacts to the levels it reads. Only the solve that converges at the nominal gmin and source scale is read, never an intermediate rung of the ladder.

Watches are read at accepted steps. A signal that crosses and crosses back inside one step is missed. The error control keeps steps short where a signal curves, and `max_step_ps` caps them. `grid_ps` does not.

Every chip kind keeps four rules. A watch reads node voltages, never a branch current. A passive chip's state holds only what its stamp reads, so every change of state takes a restart solve. An active chip keeps the rest in its core, and only a change of its drive image takes one. A chip's DC paths are the union over its states, so a path that some state opens, such as a high-impedance output, still counts for `net.floating`. Passive chips have no propagation delay, so two that flip each other at one time can have no states they agree on. Two NE555s, each OUT on the other's TRIG, reset together, then both falling OUTs set both latches while both THRES are still high. That is `chip.unsettled`.

## Active chips

An active chip runs a core on the run's clock. The NE555 is passive. The ATmega328P, kind `atmega328p`, is active, and [avr.md](avr.md) describes its adapter. The run builds one active chip per such part, in part order. An active chip's stamp reads only its drive image, one drive per output pin, and its fixed leakages. A pin drives high through its high stage from the chip's own VCC pin, drives low through its low stage into the chip's own GND pin, pulls up through its pull-up resistance from the chip's own VCC pin, or is off. A stage is a drop from its rail through a resistance. The drop is a source, so source stepping scales it. A pull-up is a resistance alone, so it does not scale. An output with a leakage resistance stamps it from the pin to the chip's own VCC pin and again to its GND pin, in every drive state, off included. A leakage is a resistance alone, so it does not scale either. The core holds everything else, and the run never compares the core.

Before each step the run asks each core where it next stops. A core stops at the first time its image may change, and at each time it samples a voltage. The step lands there. Every step therefore solves with the images in force at its start, and no accepted step is solved with a drive the core has already changed. The step's solution at a stop holds the old image, as a row there does. A sample is read on that solution, before any change at that time. If the image changed, the run takes a restart solve at the stop the way it does at a switch edge. A stop that leaves the image alone lands without one. A stop at `stop_ps` lands, and no restart runs.

A core runs ahead of the accepted time on a copy. The copy is kept when a step lands on its stop, or on a flip of one of its watches. A flip located before the copy's time drops the copy, and the core runs again from where it was last kept, to the flip. Nothing kept is ever undone. A kept copy delivers each byte its USARTs sent whose stop bit ended by its time, and drops its SPI bytes and bus phases, which no output reads yet. An active chip takes a flip at the flip's picosecond, and a flip on a restart's held solve at the restart's time. What a flip or a sample causes comes at least 1 ps later. So an active chip never reacts at the time it reads, takes no part in the settle at one time, and is never `chip.unsettled`. An active chip's logic signals are its watches' levels, recorded as a passive chip's are. A flip is recorded at its picosecond, and a flip only the restart's held solve shows at the restart's time. So a pin the core drives reads the level its own drive makes at the drive's stop. When the held solve is back across a flip the step found, the core takes both at that picosecond, and the return is recorded at the next accepted step, as for a passive chip. Recorded at one picosecond, the pair would be dropped as a zero-time glitch, and a `RESET` or brown-out pulse the core did take would not show. No restart runs at `stop_ps`, so a drive change or a source jump there is not in the run's logic signals.

Every core keeps one contract. A run up to a limit stops at its first stop after where it last reached, or reaches the limit. A run split at any limit is the same run. A clocked core counts time in whole cycles. A clock of `f` Hz runs to cycle `floor(limit_ps * f / 1e12)` for a limit of `limit_ps`, and a stop at cycle `c` is at `ceil(c * 1e12 / f)` ps. Each clock domain counts its cycles from its last frequency change, and power-on is the first. The run checks that each stop falls after where the core reached, and that no sample or input changes the image. A core that breaks the contract is an engine bug, and the run panics.

## Sessions

A session is a transient run held between calls. It opens on a netlist whose analysis is `transient`, and solves the operating point at t = 0 and the step after it. It keeps no trace, no logic history and no rating findings. An `op` analysis has no time to advance and opens no session. A circuit too large for the solver opens none, with `solver.too_large`. Neither does an operating point at t = 0 that fails to settle or gives a quantity that is not finite, and the diagnostic is the one a run reports. A grid with more rows than a trace holds still opens a session.

`advance_to` takes a time and accepts every step that lands at or before it. `stop_ps` caps the time. A running session then stands at that time, which is `now`. A time at or before `now` does nothing, so `now` never moves back. The step after the last accepted one is already solved, and it waits until a call reaches its time. A call whose time falls inside that step leaves it solved and waiting. The call never cuts the step at its time, which would make that time a breakpoint and let the call times move the steps. It never accepts the step early either, which would put `now` at a time no call asked for. So a session advanced to `stop_ps` in any number of calls takes the steps of one call. `sim run` is one such call, on a session that records every accepted step.

A probe reads every net voltage and every part's `i`, `v` and `p` at `now` in one call. On an accepted step it is that step's row, read before any restart there, and it solves nothing. Between steps it is solved from the last accepted step and retried the way a row is, as [Rows](#rows) says. So at a row time the probe is the trace's row, bit for bit. When both its solves fail, the probe is the straight line and says so. `signals` lists each chip signal, chips in part order. `levels` reads them all at `now` in that order, and `level` reads one, both with no solve. A level is the last accepted level, which is what `logic.vcd` holds at `now`. A probe and a level change nothing.

`usarts` lists each USART, chips in part order, then each chip's in datasheet order, as the part's name and the USART's, such as `USART0`. Each call ends by delivering every byte whose stop bit ended at or before the last accepted step, from the copies each core runs ahead on, and appends them to the session's serial log. A byte holds its start-bit time, the index of its USART in `usarts`, and its value. The start-bit time is the first picosecond at or after the oscillator cycle the start bit began, which is where the core stops for the `TXD` drive change. The log stops at the accepted step, as a level does, because the span from there to `now` is not settled. An input solves the held step again, and the new steps can locate a watch flip before `now` that the old step did not show. A byte known only from that span could then be one the run never sends. So a byte reaches the log once a step lands at or after the end of its stop bit, at most one step after it, and at `stop_ps` or a fault the log holds every byte that ended by `now`. The log is in the order stop bits ended, then in `usarts` order, because a byte is known only once its stop bit ends. On one USART that is also the order bytes started. With two USARTs at different bauds, a byte can start before the one ahead of it. `serial_since` takes a cursor, `SerialCursor::default()` at first, and returns the bytes after the ones it has read and the cursor past them. It solves nothing and changes nothing. A cursor converts to and from a `u64`, the count of bytes read, so it crosses a boundary that has no Rust types. A cursor past the end, from another session or a count the caller made, reads nothing and stays. Each byte is in the log once, across rewinds, restarts and inputs, so a session advanced to `stop_ps` in any number of calls holds the bytes of one call. A frame still on the wire at `stop_ps` or at a fault is not in the log. While the session runs, a session on its export, advanced to the export's stop, holds the session's bytes first, then the ones up to that stop. Once the session has ended, they are the same bytes, because a session on the export takes the steps the session took to the same end, `stop_ps` or the same fault. The step limit is the same for every run and is not in the document, so a step-limit fault is the same fault there. The log keeps every byte for the whole session, and each takes 16 bytes of memory. A USART sending without pause at 8N1 sends a byte every 10 bit times, so at 1x pacing the log grows by about 55 MB an hour at 9600 baud and 663 MB an hour at 115200. A log that doubles its buffer holds the old one and the new one at once, so in a wasm32 instance capped at 4 GiB the log fails to grow from 2^27 entries to 2^28. That is about 3.2 hours of continuous output at 115200 baud, or 39 hours at 9600. No call trims the log, and a recorded session needs every byte for `serial.csv`. `sim run` writes its session's log as `traces/serial.csv`. That is the log at `stop_ps`, or after a fault the log at the fault's `now`.

A fault is any error that ends a run after t = 0, the step limit included. It ends a session at its last accepted step, and `now` stays there. The session solves each step as soon as it accepts the one before it. So the call that accepts the last good step returns the fault, even when its time is before the step that failed, and a session can end as it opens. Every later call returns the same fault. A session that reaches `stop_ps` ends with no fault. Each call returns whether the session is running, stopped or faulted, and `fault` reads the fault.

`apply` takes an input: a part, a time and a setting. A setting is a switch's contact, a level in that field's unit, or the bytes a `serial` source sends. A voltage source reads the level as `voltage`, in volts. A current source reads it as `current`, in amperes. A potentiometer reads it as `position`, the fraction of its resistance from `a` to the wiper. A serial send is 8N1 frames at the baud the input states. The input lands at the first time from the one it asks for that is at or after `now`, after the accepted step, at or after that step's reach, and allowed after the part's last event. With no event that is 1 ps. After a level it is 2 ns later. After a switch event it is the event's bounce window and 2 ns later, so an input too close to the last one is moved, not refused. A serial send lands at the latest of that time and the end of the source's last event. The start bit is at the landing. A send asked for while those frames are still on the wire starts when the line returns to idle. The 2 ns gap a level uses does not apply to it. One `apply` appends one event and solves the held step once, however many bytes the send holds. The event goes after the part's last one. A switch event takes the bounce parse draws for an event at that index, so no earlier edge or draw moves. The held step is solved again from the accepted step with the new edge among the breakpoints, and any step that then lands by `now` is accepted. A level or a serial start bit is a jump, so that solve restarts, as it does at a switch edge. A part keeps one pending breakpoint however many inputs it takes. `apply` returns the time the input landed and the session's status after the re-solve. A fault on the re-solve ends the session at the accepted step. It refuses an input on a session that has ended, on a name no part has, on a resistor, a capacitor, a junction, a chip, or a `step` or `pulse` source, on a contact for a source or a potentiometer, on a level for a switch, on a position outside (0, 1) or a position whose leg breaks the resistance rules, on a setting that equals the value after the part's last event, a pending event included, and on one that would land at or after `stop_ps`. A serial send on a resistor, a capacitor, a junction, a chip, or a `step` or `pulse` source is refused the same way. A serial send on a switch, a potentiometer, or a plain or `steps` source is refused, and so is a contact or a level on a `serial` source. A serial send with no bytes is refused. A plain source does not become a `serial` source. A send whose frames would not end inside a time the engine can store is refused, as one that would land at or after `stop_ps` is. A refused input changes nothing.

The reach is the furthest time any attempt toward an accepted step reached. A step tries its longest first. A rejected or stalled try is shortened, a stiffened one is tried again, and a watch flip is bracketed inside the try that crossed it. So the accepted step can end before an attempt did, and before `now`. A batch run has the input's edge from t = 0, and that edge cuts every attempt past it. An edge before the reach would cut an attempt the session made, and the batch could accept a step other than the one the session kept. At or after the reach the batch makes the session's attempts, and the held step is solved the way the batch solves it. In most runs the reach is the accepted step, and an input lands at `now`, or 1 ps after it when `now` is the accepted step. After a segment start whose first step was rejected it is up to 1 ns later, and after a watch cut up to one `max_step_ps` later.

`open_document` opens a session on netlist.json bytes and keeps the document. `export` writes it back with each applied input in its part. A switch event is appended to `events`. A plain `voltage`, `current` or `position` becomes a `steps` waveform whose `initial` is that value object and whose `events` hold the applied levels. A `steps` field gains those levels on its `events`. A `serial` field gains one event per applied send, with `t_ps`, `baud`, `frame` and `bytes`. A level event carries the SI number, the field's unit and `"source": "user"`. A part with no applied input is left as it was. A session `open` made from a netlist has no document to export. The export is reprinted, with keys sorted and numbers rewritten, and its parts and nets keep their order. While the session runs, the export's `stop_ps` is the held step's reach, raised to `max_step_ps` and capped at the document's `stop_ps`, and `grid_ps` is cut to it. That is at most one `max_step_ps` past `now`. A batch run of the export takes every step the session accepted and the step it holds, bit for bit, then takes its own steps to its stop. A `stop_ps` at `now` would be a breakpoint at `now`, which splits the run where an input there would. Once the session has ended the export keeps the document's `stop_ps`, and a batch run of it takes every step the session took, to the same fault.

`record_document` opens a recorded session on netlist.json bytes. It keeps the trace, the logic history and the rating findings `sim run` keeps, and the floating-net warnings `sim run` reports. It refuses what `open_document` refuses, and also a grid with more rows than a trace holds, with `trace.too_large`, where `sim run` writes a folder that reports it. `simulate` opens its transient run the same way. `finish` consumes a recorded session, advances it to `stop_ps`, and returns the run folder `sim run` writes for the netlist the session ran, stamped with the `created_at` the caller passes. With no input applied that netlist is the bytes `record_document` read, as `sim run` writes its input unchanged, and an export would reprint them. Once an input is applied it is the export of the ended session, whose `stop_ps` is the document's, and `sim run` on that export writes the same folder byte for byte. An export taken while the session runs has a shorter `stop_ps`, so it is not that netlist. A faulted session finishes to the folder that reports its fault. A session `open` or `open_document` made records nothing, and `finish` returns no folder for it.

## Accuracy

`grid_ps` sets how dense the trace is and nothing else. `max_step_ps` caps the step. Neither sets the tolerance. Each trapezoidal step above the 1 ps floor holds that capacitor's local truncation error under 1e-5 of its own swing plus 1 nV. The step after every edge starts from the edge's restart solve, and an edge that moves a capacitor starts a segment there. That bound is per step and per capacitor. The error the steps accumulate is measured separately. On one mode it stays under 1e-4 of the swing. On an exponential, the trapezoidal rule's error over what is left of the swing grows by `x^3 / 12` a step, `x` being the step over the time constant. A step that rides 1e-5 of the whole swing has `x = 0.9 * (12e-5 * e^(t / tau))^(1/3)`. Its error peaks about 1.6 time constants after the edge at `0.039 * (12e-5)^(2/3)`, 9.5e-5 of the swing. A step never reads more than the whole swing, so this bounds one exponential. Measured from 100 ps to 1 ms, the worst at any step is 5.8e-5 to 7.2e-5. A step capped at `h` by `max_step_ps` has `x = h / tau`, and its error peaks one time constant after the edge at `x^2 / (12e)` of the swing. A swing under 100 uV is held to the 1 nV floor instead. A 100 uV edge, where 1e-5 of the swing equals that floor, measures 1.2e-4 of its swing. The tolerance holds a small swing on a large level to its own size. A 10.6 mV sag on a 5 V rail is 7.1e-5 of the sag off. The RC example's rows are 1.2e-6 V from its closed form, 4.9e-7 of the 2.5 V span. Its 20 us `max_step_ps` caps the 5 ms charge at 0.004 of the time constant, and `x^2 / (12e)` of the span is that miss. A probed row is one more step from the last accepted point, no longer than the step that accepts after it, so it carries the error of the steps around it. `examples/v-pulse-rc` has a 1 us grid and its `max_step_ps` is its 100 us time constant, so the tolerance alone sets its steps. Its rows are 7.1e-5 V from the closed form, 8.2e-5 of the 0.87 V span, under the 9.5e-5 of one exponential. A 10 ns discharge after an edge stays under 1e-4. Where modes couple, the accumulated error can exceed the one-mode figure, because a capacitor carries part of a neighbour's error. In 460 three-capacitor ladders from random states, the worst capacitor was 1.2e-4 of its own swing off. Engine 0.1.0 held every step to `1e-6 * max(|v0|, |v1|) + 1e-9` V, and on those ladders its worst capacitor was 8.8e-4 of its own swing off. A threshold crossing moves by the error over the slope there, up to `1e-4 * tau * swing / left`, `left` being what is left of the swing at the threshold. A located chip event carries that shift. The driven-CONT astable's period moves by 1.5e-4. The OUT edges of `examples/ne555-led` move by 5.49e-6 of their time at the largest. The tolerance change accounts for 5.39e-6 of that move. The later probe-row change accounts for the rest.

A step at the floor is accepted over its tolerance, so a time constant under about 43 ps is not held to it. A discharge after an edge measures this, as its worst error over its swing:

| Time constant | Worst error |
| --- | --- |
| 1 ps | 0.13 |
| 10 ps | 1.8e-2 |
| 30 ps | 6.0e-3 |
| 50 ps | 1.7e-4 |
| 100 ps | 6.6e-5 |
| 300 ps | 7.2e-5 |
| 1 ns and up | 6.9e-5 |

Under about 43 ps the capacitor turns stiff, and the error is about 0.18 times 1 ps over the time constant. Near 50 ps the steps sit at the floor. From 100 ps up the capacitor stays trapezoidal, and the error is what its steps accumulate, about 7e-5. The restart's hold shifts the start of the discharge by 1 fs over the time constant, as a fraction of the swing. A stiff capacitor whose voltage keeps moving after its mode dies goes back to the trapezoidal rule. 10 pF dumped through 2 ohm into 1 nF, then both charged through 1 Mohm, stays under 1e-4 of its closed form, at 6.4e-5. So does a 10 ns discharge beside a 30 ps mode switched in, at 4.8e-5. A capacitor whose step never reaches 256 ps stays stiff to the next segment start. It keeps backward Euler's error, which accumulates with one sign. It also takes more steps, since that error grows with the square of the step. A capacitor on a separate node keeps its accuracy beside it, and one that shares its slow mode shares its error. Two 10 pF coupled through 1 ohm and charged through 900 ohm, with a common mode of 18 ns, stay stiff and take about thirty times the steps of one 20 pF capacitor, which takes the swing's longer trapezoidal steps. While the common mode moves they are up to 2.3e-4 of their swing off, and 3.9e-5 after 100 ns. Halving `max_step_ps` and comparing the traces is still a check for a circuit with no closed form.
