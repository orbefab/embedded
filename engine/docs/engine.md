# Engine

This page describes the engine at its `engine_version`. It is not part of the netlist or the run folder format. [netlist.md](netlist.md) says what a document means, and this page says how the engine computes it. A change here moves `engine_version`, as [versions.md](versions.md) says.

## Transient steps

The run solves the operating point at t = 0, then steps to `stop_ps`. Every switch edge and every source jump before `stop_ps` is a breakpoint, and so is every stop of an active chip's core, as [Active chips](#active-chips) says. So is 1 ns after t = 0, and 1 ns after each edge or jump, when that time is before `stop_ps`. So is every row time, each multiple of `grid_ps`. A step lands on each breakpoint and on `stop_ps`. Only the row times are written to the trace. Every accepted step also feeds the envelope. A row closes the interval open since the previous row. A step that is not a row folds into that interval. A restart solve, a step the error control refused, and a probe locating a chip event are not samples. The accepted step the probe lands is.

At each switch edge or source jump the step's solution holds the old contact or level. Then the run solves once more at that time, with the new contacts and levels in force and every capacitor held at its voltage, the way an `initial` voltage is held at t = 0. This restart solve gives the currents just after the change. It is never a row, and the next step starts from it. Each held voltage moves by the capacitor's current times 1 fs over its capacitance. A jump at `stop_ps` gets no restart.

The run reads each part's edges one at a time, in time order, so it never lists a schedule whole. A dense schedule costs steps, not rows. The row cap counts grid rows only. A run accepts at most 100000000 steps, not counting t = 0. The limit sits far above the steps a run whose rows fit needs on ordinary circuits. A release build reaches it in about 40 s on a resistive pulse and about 70 s on a 1 MHz pin into a 10 ns load. At this step policy a 1 MHz pin into a 10 ns load costs about 171000 steps per simulated ms. The limit allows about 580 ms of such a pin. The step policy, not this limit, bounds an MCU-rate run. Reaching the limit before `stop_ps` ends the run with `solver.step_limit` at the last accepted time.

Solver tests that need every accepted step use `simulate_steps`, behind the test-only `step-rows` feature. A `sim` build does not have it. The test-only step-rows mode can also stop mid-run with `trace.too_large`, with `t_ps` the step that did not fit, keeping its rows.

Landing on the rows costs steps where the error control wants longer ones. A 0 to 3.3 V pulse at 1 MHz into a 10 ns RC, over 2 ms with a 1 us `max_step_ps`, takes 340001 steps with no grid inside the run. A 100 ns grid adds 4.7%, and a 10 ns grid adds 47%. A grid on the pulse's own 1 us period adds none, because every row time is already an edge. Landings also move a located chip event. `examples/ne555-led` on a grid equal to its `max_step_ps` has its OUT edges up to 5.3e-7 of their time from where steps that do not land would put them. A grid finer than `max_step_ps` shortens every step the way a smaller `max_step_ps` would. At a 1 ms grid in place of 10 ms the same edges move by up to 2.9e-5 of their time. With `max_step_ps` at 1 ms, a 100 us grid moves them by 3e-7.

The step policy is fixed. A segment starts at t = 0 and at every restart. Its first step wants 1 ns and is backward Euler. Every later step in it is trapezoidal, except on a stiff capacitor. Each step's local truncation error comes from the capacitor currents the solve already has. Backward Euler's is `h/2 * |i1 - i0| / C`. The trapezoidal rule's is `h^3 * |DD2(i)| / (6C)`, the second divided difference over the segment's last three points. The tolerance on the voltage across each capacitor is 1e-5 of its swing plus 1e-9 V. The swing is how far that voltage moves in the segment. It is the larger of two figures. One is the range of the voltage over the segment's points and the step's end. The other is the least distance to its asymptote that a step of the segment has read. A step reads `v'^2 / |v''|`, which is `h * i0^2 / (C * |i1 - i0|)` over the step, at most the circuit's span. The span is the highest net voltage minus the lowest, ground's 0 V included, and the first step reads it once from the restart. On an exponential `v'^2 / |v''|` is the distance to the asymptote, so an edge's first step knows the edge's swing before the voltage has moved. While 1e-5 of the swing dominates the 1 nV floor, that step is held to the change in its current, `(i1 - i0)^2 <= 2e-5 * i0^2`. Where two modes cancel the curvature at a restart, the first step reads past the swing, and a later step reads less. A capacitor with no current at the restart heads nowhere, and its swing is its own range. A stiff capacitor keeps Newton's tolerance, `1e-6 * max(|v0|, |v1|) + 1e-9` V.

A step over its tolerance on any capacitor is rejected and retried from the same accepted point. The next try is `0.9 * ratio^(-1/(p+1))` of it, between 0.25 and 0.9, where `ratio` is the worst error over its tolerance and `p` is 1 if that capacitor took backward Euler and 2 if it took the trapezoidal rule. An accepted step grows by the same factor, at most 2, up to `max_step_ps`. When the factor is over 1 it grows by at least 1 ps. A step of 1 ps is accepted whatever its error, unless a capacitor on the trapezoidal rule is over Newton's tolerance there. That capacitor holds a mode faster than any step, on which the trapezoidal rule rings. It turns stiff, and the step is retried. Stiffness is judged on Newton's tolerance, not the swing's. A 20 to 43 ps mode that the swing's share would let through at 1 ps would be left ringing once the step grows past it. A stiff capacitor's backward Euler error also accumulates with one sign. A stiff capacitor takes backward Euler, which damps that mode. The other capacitors keep the trapezoidal rule. Once a stiff capacitor's accepted step reaches 256 ps, the next step tries it on the trapezoidal rule. The try holds only if each tried capacitor's error is under 1e-3 of Newton's tolerance, because what is left of the mode does not decay on the trapezoidal rule. Otherwise the step is retried with them on backward Euler, and the next try waits for a step twice as long. A restart clears every stiff capacitor. An accepted step cut short by a breakpoint or by `stop_ps` keeps the step it wanted. A rejected one does not, and its retry grows from the shorter step. With no capacitor there is no error, so the step doubles. A step whose Newton solve fails is halved and retried from the same accepted point, down to 1 ps. A failure at 1 ps ends the run with `solver.convergence`.

## Chip events

A chip reads its inputs through watches. A watch is a sum of node voltages times fixed coefficients, minus an offset, and it reads high only above 0. The NE555's are TRIG minus its lower ladder node, THRES minus CONT, and RESET minus GND minus `reset_threshold`.

After each accepted step the run reads every watch on the step's solution. If any reads differently than at the last accepted point, the run locates the first picosecond where one does. Each probe re-solves the same step, from the same point with the same method, shortened to the probe time. The probe follows regula falsi on the earliest watch to flip, rounded to a whole picosecond inside the bracket. It turns to bisection once one end has moved twice in a row, and it stops at a 1 ps bracket. The located point is the later end, the first solved picosecond where a watch reads flipped, and the step lands there. A regula falsi probe whose solve stalls is replaced by the bracket's midpoint. A midpoint that stalls ends the run with `solver.convergence`, and a singular probe ends it with `solver.singular`.

A watch cuts the step; only a stamp change restarts. At the located point each chip reacts to its levels. If a chip's new state stamps differently, or a switch edge or source jump falls there, the run restarts at that point the way it does at a switch edge. The step's solution there holds the chips' old states, and so does a row at that time. The held solve after it is never a row. A flip that changes no drive still gets its edge at its picosecond, and the segment, its method and its error history continue across it. In `examples/ne555-led`, THRES drops back under CONT 1 ps after the latch resets, which is such a flip.

Every watch that flips at one picosecond, on every chip, reacts at once against the levels before it, chips in part order. The run then reads the levels on the solve with the new states and reacts again, up to 8 solves. Chips that still change after the 8th are `chip.unsettled`. The logic levels recorded at the point are the ones the chips reacted to. A watch that flipped on the step keeps its flipped level there, and every other watch takes its level on the solve with the new states. When that solve is back across a flipped watch's threshold, the return is recorded at the next accepted step. A monostable with THRES on DISCH does this, since the discharge pulls THRES back down through the hold. Every located event is at a later whole picosecond, so events cannot pile up at one time. An event located at `stop_ps`, or in the step that ends there, is recorded. No restart runs at `stop_ps`.

The operating point at t = 0 settles each chip the same way. It starts from the chip's power-on state, the NE555's latch reset, and reacts to the levels it reads. Only the solve that converges at the nominal gmin and source scale is read, never an intermediate rung of the ladder.

Watches are read at accepted steps. A signal that crosses and crosses back inside one step is missed. The error control keeps steps short where a signal curves, and `max_step_ps` and `grid_ps` cap them.

Every chip kind keeps four rules. A watch reads node voltages, never a branch current. A passive chip's state holds only what its stamp reads, so every change of state restarts. An active chip keeps the rest in its core, and only a change of its drive image restarts. A chip's DC paths are the union over its states, so a path that some state opens, such as a high-impedance output, still counts for `net.floating`. Passive chips have no propagation delay, so two that flip each other at one time can have no states they agree on. Two NE555s, each OUT on the other's TRIG, reset together, then both falling OUTs set both latches while both THRES are still high. That is `chip.unsettled`.

## Active chips

An active chip runs a core on the run's clock. The NE555 is passive, and no netlist kind is active yet. The ATmega328P core sits behind the seam, and [avr.md](avr.md) describes its adapter. An active chip's stamp reads only its drive image, one drive per output pin. A pin drives high through its high stage from the chip's own VCC pin, drives low through its low stage into the chip's own GND pin, pulls up through its pull-up resistance from the chip's own VCC pin, or is off. A stage is a drop from its rail through a resistance. The drop is a source, so source stepping scales it. A pull-up is a resistance alone, so it does not scale. The core holds everything else, and the run never compares the core.

Before each step the run asks each core where it next stops. A core stops at the first time its image may change, and at each time it samples a voltage. The step lands there. Every step therefore solves with the images in force at its start, and no accepted step is solved with a drive the core has already changed. The step's solution at a stop holds the old image, as a row there does. A sample is read on that solution, before any change at that time. If the image changed, the run restarts at the stop the way it does at a switch edge. A stop that leaves the image alone lands without a restart. A stop at `stop_ps` lands, and no restart runs.

A core runs ahead of the accepted time on a copy. The copy is kept when a step lands on its stop, or on a flip of one of its watches. A flip located before the copy's time drops the copy, and the core runs again from where it was last kept, to the flip. Nothing kept is ever undone. An active chip takes a flip at the flip's picosecond, and a flip on a restart's held solve at the restart's time. What a flip or a sample causes comes at least 1 ps later. So an active chip never reacts at the time it reads, takes no part in the settle at one time, and is never `chip.unsettled`. An active chip has no logic signals yet.

Every core keeps one contract. A run up to a limit stops at its first stop after where it last reached, or reaches the limit. A run split at any limit is the same run. A clocked core counts time in whole cycles. A clock of `f` Hz runs to cycle `floor(limit_ps * f / 1e12)` for a limit of `limit_ps`, and a stop at cycle `c` is at `ceil(c * 1e12 / f)` ps. Each clock domain counts its cycles from its last frequency change, and power-on is the first. The run checks that each stop falls after where the core reached, and that no sample or input changes the image. A core that breaks the contract is an engine bug, and the run panics.

## Accuracy

`grid_ps` sets how dense the trace is. `max_step_ps` caps the step, and so does `grid_ps`, since a step lands on every row. Neither sets the tolerance. Each trapezoidal step above the 1 ps floor holds that capacitor's local truncation error under 1e-5 of its own swing plus 1 nV, from a consistent restart after every edge. That bound is per step and per capacitor. The error the steps accumulate is measured separately. On one mode it stays under 1e-4 of the swing. On an exponential, the trapezoidal rule's error over what is left of the swing grows by `x^3 / 12` a step, `x` being the step over the time constant. A step that rides 1e-5 of the whole swing has `x = 0.9 * (12e-5 * e^(t / tau))^(1/3)`. Its error peaks about 1.6 time constants after the edge at `0.039 * (12e-5)^(2/3)`, 9.5e-5 of the swing. A step never reads more than the whole swing, so this bounds one exponential. Measured from 100 ps to 1 ms, the worst at any step is 5.8e-5 to 7.2e-5. A step capped at `h` by `max_step_ps` or the grid has `x = h / tau`, and its error peaks one time constant after the edge at `x^2 / (12e)` of the swing. A swing under 100 uV is held to the 1 nV floor instead. A 100 uV edge, where 1e-5 of the swing equals that floor, measures 1.2e-4 of its swing. The tolerance holds a small swing on a large level to its own size. A 10.6 mV sag on a 5 V rail is 7.1e-5 of the sag off. The RC example's rows are 1.2e-6 V from its closed form, 4.9e-7 of the 2.5 V span. Its 20 us grid caps the 5 ms charge at 0.004 of the time constant, and `x^2 / (12e)` of the span is that miss. A 10 ns discharge after an edge stays under 1e-4. Where modes couple, the accumulated error can exceed the one-mode figure, because a capacitor carries part of a neighbour's error. In 460 three-capacitor ladders from random states, the worst capacitor was 1.2e-4 of its own swing off. Engine 0.1.0 held every step to `1e-6 * max(|v0|, |v1|) + 1e-9` V, and on those ladders its worst capacitor was 8.8e-4 of its own swing off. A threshold crossing moves by the error over the slope there, up to `1e-4 * tau * swing / left`, `left` being what is left of the swing at the threshold. A located chip event carries that shift. The driven-CONT astable's period moves by 1.5e-4, and the OUT edges of `examples/ne555-led` by up to 5.4e-6 of their time.

A step at the floor is accepted over its tolerance, so a time constant under about 43 ps is not held to it. A discharge after an edge measures this, as its worst error over its swing:

| Time constant | Worst error |
| --- | --- |
| 1 ps | 0.13 |
| 10 ps | 1.8e-2 |
| 30 ps | 6.0e-3 |
| 50 ps | 1.7e-4 |
| 100 ps | 6.6e-5 |
| 300 ps | 7.2e-5 |
| 1 ns and up | 6.9e-5 |

Under about 43 ps the capacitor turns stiff, and the error is about 0.18 times 1 ps over the time constant. Near 50 ps the steps sit at the floor. From 100 ps up the capacitor stays trapezoidal, and the error is what its steps accumulate, about 7e-5. The restart's hold shifts the start of the discharge by 1 fs over the time constant, as a fraction of the swing. A stiff capacitor whose voltage keeps moving after its mode dies goes back to the trapezoidal rule. 10 pF dumped through 2 ohm into 1 nF, then both charged through 1 Mohm, stays under 1e-4 of its closed form, at 6.4e-5. So does a 10 ns discharge beside a 30 ps mode switched in, at 4.8e-5. A capacitor whose step never reaches 256 ps stays stiff to the next restart. It keeps backward Euler's error, which accumulates with one sign. It also takes more steps, since that error grows with the square of the step. A capacitor on a separate node keeps its accuracy beside it, and one that shares its slow mode shares its error. Two 10 pF coupled through 1 ohm and charged through 900 ohm, with a common mode of 18 ns, stay stiff and take about thirty times the steps of one 20 pF capacitor, which takes the swing's longer trapezoidal steps. While the common mode moves they are up to 2.3e-4 of their swing off, and 3.9e-5 after 100 ns. Halving `max_step_ps` and comparing the traces is still a check for a circuit with no closed form.
