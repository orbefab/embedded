# Engine

This page describes the engine at its `engine_version`. It is not part of the netlist or the run folder format. [netlist.md](netlist.md) says what a document means, and this page says how the engine computes it. A change here moves `engine_version`, as [versions.md](versions.md) says.

## Transient steps

The run solves the operating point at t = 0, then steps to `stop_ps`. Every switch edge and every source jump before `stop_ps` is a breakpoint. So is 1 ns after t = 0, and 1 ns after each edge or jump, when that time is before `stop_ps`. So is every row time, each multiple of `grid_ps`. A step lands on each breakpoint and on `stop_ps`. Only the row times are written to the trace. Every accepted step also feeds the envelope. A row closes the interval open since the previous row. A step that is not a row folds into that interval. A restart solve, a step the error control refused, and a probe locating a chip event are not samples. The accepted step the probe lands is.

At each switch edge or source jump the step's solution holds the old contact or level. Then the run solves once more at that time, with the new contacts and levels in force and every capacitor held at its voltage, the way an `initial` voltage is held at t = 0. This restart solve gives the currents just after the change. It is never a row, and the next step starts from it. Each held voltage moves by the capacitor's current times 1 fs over its capacitance. A jump at `stop_ps` gets no restart.

The run reads each part's edges one at a time, in time order, so it never lists a schedule whole. A dense schedule costs steps, not rows. The row cap counts grid rows only. A run accepts at most 100000000 steps, not counting t = 0. The limit sits far above the steps a run whose rows fit needs on ordinary circuits. A release build reaches it in about 40 s on a resistive pulse and about 70 s on a 1 MHz pin into a 10 ns load. At this step policy a 1 MHz pin into a 10 ns load costs about 700000 steps per simulated ms. The limit allows about 140 ms of such a pin. The step policy, not this limit, bounds an MCU-rate run. Reaching the limit before `stop_ps` ends the run with `solver.step_limit` at the last accepted time.

Solver tests that need every accepted step use `simulate_steps`, behind the test-only `step-rows` feature. A `sim` build does not have it. The test-only step-rows mode can also stop mid-run with `trace.too_large`, with `t_ps` the step that did not fit, keeping its rows.

Landing on the rows costs steps where the error control wants longer ones. A 0 to 3.3 V pulse at 1 MHz into a 10 ns RC, over 2 ms with a 1 us `max_step_ps`, takes 1447286 steps with no grid inside the run. A 100 ns grid adds 0.7%, and a 10 ns grid adds 10%. A grid on the pulse's own 1 us period adds none, because every row time is already an edge. Landings also move a located chip event. `examples/ne555-led` on a grid equal to its `max_step_ps` has its OUT edges up to 1.2e-6 of their time from where steps that do not land would put them. A grid finer than `max_step_ps` shortens every step the way a smaller `max_step_ps` would. At a 1 ms grid in place of 10 ms the same edges move by up to 2.7e-5 of their time. With `max_step_ps` at 1 ms, a 100 us grid moves them by 3e-7.

The step policy is fixed. A segment starts at t = 0 and at every restart. Its first step wants 1 ns and is backward Euler. Every later step in it is trapezoidal, except on a stiff capacitor. Each step's local truncation error comes from the capacitor currents the solve already has. Backward Euler's is `h/2 * |i1 - i0| / C`. The trapezoidal rule's is `h^3 * |DD2(i)| / (6C)`, the second divided difference over the segment's last three points. The tolerance is `1e-6 * max(|v0|, |v1|) + 1e-9` V, Newton's own, on the voltage across each capacitor.

A step over its tolerance on any capacitor is rejected and retried from the same accepted point. The next try is `0.9 * ratio^(-1/(p+1))` of it, between 0.25 and 0.9, where `ratio` is the worst error over its tolerance and `p` is 1 if that capacitor took backward Euler and 2 if it took the trapezoidal rule. An accepted step grows by the same factor, at most 2, up to `max_step_ps`. When the factor is over 1 it grows by at least 1 ps. A backward Euler step of 1 ps is accepted whatever its error. A capacitor whose trapezoidal step of 1 ps is over its tolerance holds a mode faster than any step, on which the trapezoidal rule rings. It turns stiff, and the step is retried. A stiff capacitor takes backward Euler, which damps that mode. The other capacitors keep the trapezoidal rule. Once a stiff capacitor's accepted step reaches 256 ps, the next step tries it on the trapezoidal rule. The try holds only if each tried capacitor's error is under 1e-3 of its tolerance, because what is left of the mode does not decay on the trapezoidal rule. Otherwise the step is retried with them on backward Euler, and the next try waits for a step twice as long. A restart clears every stiff capacitor. An accepted step cut short by a breakpoint or by `stop_ps` keeps the step it wanted. A rejected one does not, and its retry grows from the shorter step. With no capacitor there is no error, so the step doubles. A step whose Newton solve fails is halved and retried from the same accepted point, down to 1 ps. A failure at 1 ps ends the run with `solver.convergence`.

## Chip events

A chip reads its inputs through watches. A watch is a sum of node voltages times fixed coefficients, minus an offset, and it reads high only above 0. The NE555's are TRIG minus its lower ladder node, THRES minus CONT, and RESET minus GND minus `reset_threshold`.

After each accepted step the run reads every watch on the step's solution. If any reads differently than at the last accepted point, the run locates the first picosecond where one does. Each probe re-solves the same step, from the same point with the same method, shortened to the probe time. The probe follows regula falsi on the earliest watch to flip, rounded to a whole picosecond inside the bracket. It turns to bisection once one end has moved twice in a row, and it stops at a 1 ps bracket. The located point is the later end, the first solved picosecond where a watch reads flipped, and the step lands there. A regula falsi probe whose solve stalls is replaced by the bracket's midpoint. A midpoint that stalls ends the run with `solver.convergence`, and a singular probe ends it with `solver.singular`.

A watch cuts the step; only a stamp change restarts. At the located point each chip reacts to its levels. If a chip's new state stamps differently, or a switch edge or source jump falls there, the run restarts at that point the way it does at a switch edge. The step's solution there holds the chips' old states, and so does a row at that time. The held solve after it is never a row. A flip that changes no drive still gets its edge at its picosecond, and the segment, its method and its error history continue across it. In `examples/ne555-led`, THRES drops back under CONT 1 ps after the latch resets, which is such a flip.

Every watch that flips at one picosecond, on every chip, reacts at once against the levels before it, chips in part order. The run then reads the levels on the solve with the new states and reacts again, up to 8 solves. Chips that still change after the 8th are `chip.unsettled`. The logic levels recorded at the point are the ones the chips reacted to. A watch that flipped on the step keeps its flipped level there, and every other watch takes its level on the solve with the new states. When that solve is back across a flipped watch's threshold, the return is recorded at the next accepted step. A monostable with THRES on DISCH does this, since the discharge pulls THRES back down through the hold. Every located event is at a later whole picosecond, so events cannot pile up at one time. An event located at `stop_ps`, or in the step that ends there, is recorded. No restart runs at `stop_ps`.

The operating point at t = 0 settles each chip the same way. It starts from the chip's power-on state, the NE555's latch reset, and reacts to the levels it reads. Only the solve that converges at the nominal gmin and source scale is read, never an intermediate rung of the ladder.

Watches are read at accepted steps. A signal that crosses and crosses back inside one step is missed. The error control keeps steps short where a signal curves, and `max_step_ps` and `grid_ps` cap them.

Every chip kind keeps four rules. A watch reads node voltages, never a branch current. A chip's state holds only what its stamp reads, so every change of state restarts. A chip with memory that leaves its outputs alone would need its own rule for when to restart. A chip's DC paths are the union over its states, so a path that some state opens, such as a high-impedance output, still counts for `net.floating`. Chips have no propagation delay, so two that flip each other at one time can have no states they agree on. Two NE555s, each OUT on the other's TRIG, reset together, then both falling OUTs set both latches while both THRES are still high. That is `chip.unsettled`.

## Accuracy

`grid_ps` sets how dense the trace is. `max_step_ps` caps the step, and so does `grid_ps`, since a step lands on every row. Neither sets the tolerance. Each step above the 1 ps floor holds every capacitor's local truncation error under 1e-6 of its voltage plus 1 nV, from a consistent restart after every edge. Error accumulates over steps. The RC example stays under 1e-6 of span of its closed form, and a 10 ns discharge after an edge under 2e-5.

A step at the floor is accepted over its tolerance, so a time constant under about 43 ps is not held to it. A discharge after an edge measures this, as its worst error over its swing:

| Time constant | Worst error |
| --- | --- |
| 1 ps | 0.13 |
| 10 ps | 1.8e-2 |
| 30 ps | 6.0e-3 |
| 50 ps | 1.7e-4 |
| 100 ps | 3.9e-5 |
| 300 ps | 1.1e-5 |
| 1 ns and up | 1.3e-5 |

Under about 43 ps the capacitor turns stiff, and the error is about 0.18 times 1 ps over the time constant. Above it the capacitor stays trapezoidal, and the error comes from its first step. The restart's hold shifts the start of the discharge by 1 fs over the time constant, as a fraction of the swing. A stiff capacitor whose voltage keeps moving after its mode dies goes back to the trapezoidal rule. 10 pF dumped through 2 ohm into 1 nF, then both charged through 1 Mohm, stays under 2e-5 of its closed form. So does a 10 ns discharge beside a 30 ps mode switched in. A capacitor whose step never reaches 256 ps stays stiff to the next restart. It keeps backward Euler's error, which accumulates with one sign. It also takes more steps, since that error grows with the square of the step. A capacitor on a separate node keeps its accuracy beside it, and one that shares its slow mode shares its error. Two 10 pF coupled through 1 ohm and charged through 900 ohm, with a common mode of 18 ns, stay stiff and take about ten times the steps of one capacitor. Once the mode has died they are 6.7e-7 of their swing off. Halving `max_step_ps` and comparing the traces is still a check for a circuit with no closed form.
