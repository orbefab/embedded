# ESP32-S3 SoC

`crates/esp32`, package `sim-esp32`, is the ESP32-S3 around the `sim-xtensa` core. Its `s3` module is the chip. The circuit crate holds it behind the active chip seam, and each `esp32s3` part of a netlist runs one. [netlist.md](netlist.md#esp32-s3) describes the part.

The register addresses and bit positions come from IDF's `soc/esp32s3` headers. The electrical values in `examples/esp32s3-blink` come from the ESP32-S3 Series Datasheet v2.2, Table 5-4.

## What it models

The chip starts as a bare-metal ELF leaves it. Every loadable segment is in SRAM, the PC is at the entry, and the rest of the core is in its reset state. There is no boot ROM, no flash and no cache. The core has one clock, the 40 MHz crystal, so `CCOUNT`, SYSTIMER and the watchdogs count the same cycles. A cycle is 25 ns. Only core 0 runs.

- **SRAM.** 512 KB, seen through two windows. From `0x4037_0000` to `0x403e_0000` on the instruction bus it is SRAM from byte 0. From `0x3fc8_8000` to `0x3fd0_0000` on the data bus it is SRAM from byte `0x8000`. So `0x4037_8000` and `0x3fc8_8000` are one byte. The core fetches only through the instruction window, and loads and stores through either. Through the instruction window only an aligned word goes, as on the board. A byte or halfword access, and an `l32ai`, `s32ri` or `s32c1i`, raises `LoadStoreError`, cause 3. An unaligned access raises `LoadStoreAlignment`, cause 9. The access's address goes to `EXCVADDR`, and a store writes nothing.
- **GPIO, IO_MUX and the GPIO matrix's output side.** A pad drives from the matrix when its IO_MUX function is the GPIO one. That is function 1 on every pad, or function 0 on a pad whose function 0 is its GPIO: GPIO0 to GPIO21, GPIO33 to GPIO38, GPIO45 and GPIO46. Signal 256 in `GPIO_FUNCn_OUT_SEL_CFG` is the pad's own `GPIO_OUT` bit, enabled by its `GPIO_ENABLE` bit. `OUT_INV_SEL`, `OEN_SEL` and `OEN_INV_SEL` act as the headers say, and with `OEN_SEL` clear the peripheral enables the pad, as SPI2 does each of its outputs. A pad routed to an SPI2 output drives that signal's idle level, as the SPI2 entry below says. A pad routed to another signal drives nothing, because no other peripheral output is modelled. A pad that does not drive pulls up while its `FUN_WPU` is set. `FUN_WPD` is kept and not stamped, since the seam has no pull-down. `GPIO_PINn`, which holds the open-drain bit and the interrupt setup, is not modelled. `GPIO_IN` and `GPIO_IN1` read 0 on a pad whose `FUN_IE` is clear. A pad with a net reads its net, a pad with no net reads what it drives, or its pull-up. The input matrix registers are kept and route nothing.
- **SYSTIMER as a counter.** It counts at 16 MHz, 2 counts every 5 cycles. Unit 0 works from power-on and unit 1 does not, as `SYSTIMER_CONF` resets. `UPDATE`, the value registers, `VALUE_VALID`, the load registers and the load strobes work. Alarms and interrupts are not modelled.
- **The watchdogs.** The RTC watchdog and the main watchdog of each timer group. Each runs while its `EN` bit is set and its first stage acts, or while its flash boot bit is set. That bit is set at power-on on the RTC watchdog and on timer group 0's, as the boot ROM leaves them. Timer group 0's first stage then runs out at 26,000,000 cycles, 0.65 s, and the RTC watchdog's at 400,000 cycles of a 136 kHz slow clock, about 2.94 s. That is its `WDTCONFIG1`, 200,000 at power-on, times 2, the multiplier for eFuse `WDT_DELAY_SEL` 0. eFuses are not modelled, so the multiplier stays 2. Every write but the one to the write-protect register needs `0x50d83aa1` there, and the key is in place at power-on. A write takes effect at once, so `TIMG_WDT_CONF_UPDATE_EN` is not needed, and a hold written below the count runs out at the write. A write to the feed register restarts the count. The super watchdog's two registers are kept, and start unlocked with `SWD_SIGNAL_WIDTH` 300 as on the chip. It never runs out. A watchdog that runs out its first stage ends the run with `chip.watchdog`, whatever that stage's action.
- **SPI2 in its CPU-buffer mode.** The firmware writes up to 64 bytes into `SPI_W0` to `SPI_W15` and sets `SPI_USR`. SPI2 then sends a command phase of `SPI_USR_COMMAND_BITLEN` + 1 bits, from bit 7 of `SPI_USR_COMMAND_VALUE` down to bit 0 and then from bit 15 down to bit 8, an address phase of the top `SPI_USR_ADDR_BITLEN` + 1 bits of `SPI_ADDR`, and `SPI_MS_DATA_BITLEN` + 1 bits of data, each when its `SPI_USR_*` bit is set and each on one, two or four lanes as `SPI_FCMD_*`, `SPI_FADDR_*` and `SPI_FWRITE_*` say. It lasts `SPI_CS_SETUP_TIME` + 1 clocks when `SPI_CS_SETUP` is set, each phase's bits over its lanes, rounded up, and `SPI_CS_HOLD_TIME` clocks when `SPI_CS_HOLD` is set. Each of those counts is 0 when its enable is clear. CS setup and hold follow IDF's whole-clock reading, not the TRM's extra half clock, until a capture settles it. SPI2 has a clock only while `SPI_CLK_EN` and `SPI_MST_CLK_ACTIVE` are set in `SPI_CLK_GATE`. `spi_ll_master_init` sets `SPI_MST_CLK_ACTIVE`, and `spi_ll_enable_clock`, which `spi_bus_initialize` calls, sets `SPI_CLK_EN`. A clock is the source `SPI_MST_CLK_SEL` picks, the 80 MHz `PLL_F80M` or the 40 MHz crystal, divided as `SPI_CLOCK` says, and the length is rounded up to whole core cycles. `SPI_USR` reads 1 until the transfer ends, and `SPI_TRANS_DONE_INT_RAW` sets at its end. A write of `SPI_USR` while a transfer runs starts nothing. `SPI_UPDATE` reads 0 at once and syncs `SPI_ADDR` to `SPI_DOUT_MODE` into SPI2's clock domain, the registers IDF's `spi_ll.h` has a segmented transfer's CONF phase reload. A transfer runs on the synced registers, so a start while one of them differs from its value at the last update is refused. A store that sets both `SPI_USR` and `SPI_UPDATE` starts on the registers the update before it synced. `SPI_CS_KEEP_ACTIVE` keeps CS asserted after the transfer. CS changes only at a transfer, so clearing `SPI_CS_KEEP_ACTIVE` with no transfer after it leaves the last record held. A pad routed to `FSPICS0`, `FSPICLK`, `FSPID`, `FSPIQ`, `FSPIWP` or `FSPIHD` drives that signal's idle level for as long as it is routed, never the bits: CS deasserted, SCLK at `SPI_CK_IDLE_EDGE`, and each data line at its `SPI_*_POL` bit, which resets to 1. So a pad with a net reads its idle level in `GPIO_IN` during a transfer too, where the chip reads its CS low. A transfer started with slave mode, DMA or the segmented transfer `SPI_USR_CONF` starts, LSB-first bit order, QPI, an octal phase, DTR, a dummy phase, a read phase, `SPI_USR_MOSI_HIGHPART`, the 3-line mode of `SPI_SIO`, the full duplex of `SPI_DOUTDIN`, `SPI_CK_OUT_EDGE`, `SPI_CS0_DIS`, data that is not whole bytes or is over 64 bytes, its clock off, or a synced register not yet updated, ends the run with `chip.unsupported_setting` at its store. `SPI_CK_OUT_EDGE` gives clock mode 1 or 2, which samples on the falling edge of SCLK, and a record holds only the bits. `SPI_CONF_BITLEN` times the CONF state of a segmented transfer, so it changes no transfer the model runs. `SPI_CLK_MODE` is kept and not run. Its extra SCLK edges come after CS rises, so they change no record. SPI2 raises no interrupt. A program routing SPI2's interrupt to the core meets the interrupt matrix's `chip.unmodeled_register`.
- **The USB Serial/JTAG console.** The firmware writes bytes to `USB_SERIAL_JTAG_EP1_REG`. The unit sends them as one packet when the firmware writes `WR_DONE` to `USB_SERIAL_JTAG_EP1_CONF_REG`, or when the FIFO holds its 64th byte. The host takes each packet at once, so `SERIAL_IN_EP_DATA_FREE` always reads 1. The host sends nothing, so `SERIAL_OUT_EP_DATA_AVAIL` reads 0. The bytes reach `serial.csv` as usart `USB_SERIAL_JTAG`. Every byte of one packet starts and ends at the cycle the packet went.

No peripheral raises an interrupt. The core's own timer interrupts, `CCOMPARE0` to `CCOMPARE2`, and `WAITI` work as `sim-xtensa` models them. A core in `WAITI` skips to its next timer match, a watchdog's deadline or the run's limit.

The core runs the base ops `sim-xtensa` models, the single-precision FPU, MAC16, the Boolean registers and `s32c1i`, and holds the `q` registers and the `cop_ai` user registers as plain state. An FPU op with `CPENABLE` bit 0 clear raises cause 32, and a `cop_ai` op with bit 3 clear raises cause 35, at the exception vector. An `ee.*` vector op, or any other instruction the core does not model, ends the run with `chip.unsupported_instruction`.

## Unmodeled registers

Peripheral space runs from `0x6000_0000` to `0x600d_1000`. A register there that the model does not keep reads 0, and a write to it is dropped. The run reports each such register once, at its first access, with `chip.unmodeled_register`. The warning names the address, the register as its block and offset, such as `UART0+0x0`, whether that access was a write, the PC, and the ELF symbol at or below the PC. The blocks and their bases are IDF's `reg_base.h`. A byte or halfword access to a kept register reads, or merges into, its word.

A load or store outside SRAM and peripheral space raises `LoadStoreError`. The core takes the exception at its vector. A fetch outside the instruction window ends the run with `chip.unmapped_fetch`. An instruction the core does not model ends it with `chip.unsupported_instruction`.

## Behind the seam

Each GPIO with a net is one output and one watch, in [`Esp32s3::PINS`](netlist.md#esp32-s3) order. A store that changes the drive of a pad with a net stops the core before its next instruction. That stop is at the store's cycle, and the run lands there, as [engine.md](engine.md#active-chips) says. The drive of a pad with no net is never stamped. A watch flip sets the level that pad's `GPIO_IN` bit reads from the flip's time on. The run converts cycles to picoseconds by the clocked-core rule in [engine.md](engine.md#active-chips), at 40 MHz.

Each SPI2 transfer is a `Spi` record, as [engine.md](engine.md#records) says, that starts at the store of `SPI_USR` and ends at the transfer's end, a stop of the core. Its phases, lanes, data, clock and CS are the transfer's. Its nets are those of the pads the matrix routed CS, SCLK and D0 to D3 to when the transfer started, the lowest-numbered pad for a signal routed to several, and none for a signal on no pad with a net. In `link.csv` its `pin` is the name of the CS pad, such as `GPIO12`, or `SPI2` when CS is routed to none of the part's [pins](netlist.md#esp32-s3). A store that starts a transfer bounds the core at the transfer's end, and the run lands there. A store that SPI2 refuses stops the core before the store writes.

A fault ends the run at the stop that reports it. The run keeps every row and every byte before that time, and the diagnostic is the error that ends it.

## The examples

`examples/esp32s3-blink` runs `firmware/esp32s3/blink.c` and `firmware/esp32s3/start.S`. The program disables the watchdogs, routes GPIO21 to its `GPIO_OUT` bit, and prints its pin and its period. It then sleeps in `WAITI` while the `CCOMPARE1` interrupt toggles GPIO21 every 4,000,000 cycles, 100 ms, eight times. The interrupt notes the level `GPIO_IN` read before each edge and SYSTIMER after it. The program prints those, and the SYSTIMER span from the first edge to the last. The text it prints is in a comment at the top of `blink.c`.

`scripts/esp32s3-firmware.sh` rebuilds the ELF from those sources with `xtensa-esp32s3-elf-gcc`, GCC 14.2.0 from crosstool-NG esp-14.2.0_20260121. Another compiler builds other bytes. The script files the ELF in `examples/esp32s3-blink/blobs/` under its SHA-256 and writes that hash and its length into the netlist. With `--check` it compares a fresh build with the committed blob.

`scripts/esp32s3_blink.py --check <run>` checks a run folder apart from the engine. The expected text comes from `blink.c`, and the check first holds it to the program's closed form: edge k reads the level edge k - 1 left, and the SYSTIMER span is 7 × 4,000,000 × 2 / 5 counts. `traces/serial.csv` must hold that text byte for byte, as `u1`'s `USB_SERIAL_JTAG`, each line at one time on a cycle boundary. In `traces/logic.vcd`, GPIO21 must change eight times, rising first and alternating. Edge k must sit at cycle k × 4,000,000 + 18. The 18 is the cycles from the `CCOMPARE1` match to the store that changes the pin. It follows from the core's cycle costs and from the run landing each edge on its store's cycle, so the check fails when either moves. `scripts/check.sh` runs the check.

`examples/esp32s3-pattern` runs `firmware/esp32s3/pattern.c` and `firmware/esp32s3/pattern_start.S` on an `st77916` panel wired to GPIO11 to GPIO18 and GPIO21. The program disables the watchdogs, routes SCLK, CS and D0 to D3 to GPIO11 to GPIO16 through the matrix, and drives RST on GPIO17 and the backlight on GPIO21 as plain outputs. SPI2 runs at 10 MHz, the crystal divided by 4. The program resets the panel, sends its init commands, turns the backlight on, and draws five rectangles. Each command is an 8-bit command phase holding the opcode and a 24-bit address phase holding the command shifted left by 8, on one lane. Register parameters follow on one lane, and pixels follow on four lanes in transfers of 64 bytes, with CS kept between them. Its waits sleep in `WAITI` until a `CCOMPARE1` match. It prints `BEGIN pattern` and `END pattern`.

`firmware/esp32s3/pattern.json` holds the init commands, each with its parameters and the milliseconds the program waits after it, and the five rectangles, each with its inclusive corners and a fill rule. `scripts/pattern_header.py` writes `firmware/esp32s3/pattern.h` from it, and `--check` fails unless the committed header is a fresh write. `scripts/esp32s3-firmware.sh` builds this ELF beside the blink one and files it in `examples/esp32s3-pattern/blobs/`.

`scripts/render_st77916.py --pattern firmware/esp32s3/pattern.json --check <run>` checks a run folder apart from the engine. It builds the transfers `pattern.c` sends from `pattern.json` alone, and plays them into its own model of the panel. Each row of `traces/link.csv` must be its expected transfer, each row of `traces/display.csv` its expected pixel write, and `traces/display-lcd1.rgb565` the expected frame byte for byte. It refuses a pattern with a rectangle of one value. `scripts/check.sh` runs both checks, as [reference.md](reference.md) says.
